Where to Eat in Dartford

Quick Answer

For a fast answer: Britannia Grill on Dartford Road for the independently run kebab that locals have relied on for over twenty years, Ossie's Cafe at Copperfields for the town's best-loved full English, Cadiz Tapas on Spital Street for authentic charcoal-grilled Spanish cooking from a chef trained in Spain, and The Malt Shovel on Darenth Road for cask ale in a pub that has been here since 1673. Four moods, one north-Kent market town.

Dartford does not have one food scene so much as several, layered street by street. This guide does something simple: it picks one genuine standout in each of four everyday categories and uses them as doorways into the wider town.

The Four Picks

Takeaway

Britannia Grill

139 Dartford Road, east of the town centre

Britannia Grill has been feeding Dartford from its spot on Dartford Road for more than two decades, and its reputation as the town's best kebab house is as solid as ever. The independent business has been owned and run by the same family since its incorporation in 2001, and the consistency shows: reviewers who have been ordering here for fourteen or more years still describe the doner, chicken shish and lamb kofte as the best for miles. The food hygiene inspection in February 2026 confirmed continued high standards. There is no delivery service and no reservations, just a counter serving fresh, hot meat and bread to a loyal local following seven nights a week. The chicken shish wrap and the mixed grill are the orders to make.

Known for
Dartford's most trusted independent kebab, two decades of local loyalty
Good for
A no-fuss, generous late-evening meal collected on the way home
Cafe

Ossie's Cafe

Unit 10, Copperfields Shopping Centre, Spital Street

Ossie's is the kind of independently owned family cafe that every market town used to have and very few still do. Tucked into the Copperfields courtyard off Spital Street, it has been a fixture of Dartford's daily life for long enough to have accumulated 2,816 Tripadvisor reviews at a 4.5-star average, and a January 2026 review named it simply the best cafe in Dartford. The menu centres on hearty British breakfasts, the full English being the standout, alongside hot lunches, homemade milkshakes that draw regulars back on their own, and daily specials that include a much-praised curry. Run by Ossie and his family with a warmth that national chains cannot replicate, it opens Monday to Saturday from 7:30am and shuts at 3:30pm, so an early start is rewarded.

Known for
The best full English in Dartford, homemade milkshakes, daily specials
Good for
A proper breakfast or a cheap, cheerful lunch in the town centre
Restaurant

Cadiz Tapas Restaurant and Cocktail Bar

70 Spital Street, town centre

Cadiz Tapas brought a genuine taste of southern Spain to Dartford's Spital Street, and the kitchen's credentials are the real thing: head chef Maria has over two decades of culinary experience trained in Spain, and the menu built around her charcoal-grilled meats, meze-style sharing plates and seafood reflects that background. Owner Lorenzo runs the room with the kind of attentiveness that makes the 446-diner OpenTable rating of 4.1 stars easy to understand. Regulars single out the paella as the best they have tried outside Spain, and the tender pork cheek and the seafood boards are firm favourites. The cocktail bar adds a lively evening dimension; bookings are advised for weekends and available through the restaurant's own website.

Known for
Authentic Spanish charcoal grill, paella, pork cheek, Maria's 20-year Spanish training
Good for
A relaxed shared-plates dinner or a full Spanish evening with cocktails
Pub

The Malt Shovel

3 Darenth Road, near the town centre

The Malt Shovel has been on this spot since 1673, making it one of the oldest surviving pubs in north Kent, and the interior still carries the evidence: a low-ceilinged tap room hung with an 1880s Dartford Brewery mirror sits alongside a larger bar opening onto a conservatory and a generous beer garden. Young's bought the freehold in 1983, and their Original bitter has poured alongside St Austell Tribute and rotating guest ales ever since. The CAMRA Good Beer Guide has listed the pub every year from 2009 to the present day without a break. The kitchen serves hearty pub classics Wednesday to Sunday, and the week runs to a Monday quiz, Tuesday crib night, and a jazz jam on the first Wednesday of the month. Food served Thursday to Sunday at lunch; booking advised for Sunday roasts.

Dartford's Food Scene

Dartford has been a market town since the medieval period, sitting at the crossing of two Roman roads where the River Darent meets the road to the Thames crossing, and its Saturday market has run on the High Street for centuries, offering fruit, veg, fresh fish and hot food alongside fashion and general goods. The opening of Bluewater shopping centre in 1999, just a few minutes from the town in the old Blue Circle chalk quarry, reset the town's retail gravity, but the independent food scene has held its own in the Spital Street and Copperfields quarter.

Spital Street is the closest thing Dartford has to a restaurant row: Cadiz Tapas, the Copperfields courtyard cafes and a handful of independents sit within a short walk of each other in the compact town centre. The monthly farmers' market on the third Friday of each month adds a more artisan dimension, with local Kent producers joining the regular greengrocers. The town also has strong connections to its own culinary past: the Dartford Brewery that produced the mirror in the Malt Shovel tap room operated in the town through the Victorian era and into the twentieth century before closing.

Dartford is also known for a different kind of cultural heritage: Mick Jagger was born here in 1943, and famously met Keith Richards on Platform 2 of Dartford railway station in 1961, an encounter commemorated by a blue plaque. The town has leaned into this connection, but its quieter food story is just as worth knowing. The Malt Shovel on Darenth Road, serving ale since 1673, predates the Stones by nearly three centuries, and Ossie's Cafe at Copperfields is precisely the kind of community institution that a touring band would have survived on.

Behind the Pass: The Kitchens That Keep Dartford Fed

Every kitchen in this guide, however small, runs an extraction system - canopy, baffle filters, plenum, ductwork and fan - that pulls heat, smoke and grease-laden air off the cookline. Dartford's compact High Street and Spital Street kitchens run through lunch and dinner every day of the week, and the charcoal grills, flat-top kebab plates and tapas burners that drive the town-centre dining scene push a sustained load of grease-laden vapour into their canopies every service.

Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the "responsible person" for a premises must assess and manage fire risk, and a grease-laden duct is one of the most serious risks in any catering building. The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) reinforce the same duty of care, while an Environmental Health Officer (EHO) or a buildings insurer can ask to see documented proof the extraction system has been professionally cleaned.

What is TR19 Grease?

TR19 Grease is the UK industry standard for cleaning ductwork and extraction systems in commercial kitchens, published by the Building Engineering Services Association (BESA). It defines how grease deposits are measured, the maximum thickness allowed before cleaning is due, and the post-clean certification a kitchen should hold - the benchmark insurers, fire assessors and EHOs look for.

A small kitchen is no less of a fire risk than a big one. A clean, certified extraction system is what keeps it safe and insured.

Industry Insight

The Cleaning Frequency Most Operators Get Wrong

Here is something many venue owners do not realise: under TR19 Grease, cleaning frequency is set by how many hours the kitchen actually cooks, not by how the canopy looks. A heavy-use kitchen running 12 to 16 hours a day should be cleaned roughly every three months. Moderate use of 6 to 12 hours points to every six months, and light use of 2 to 6 hours to every twelve.

The catch is that baffle filters can look clean while the plenum, the horizontal duct runs and the fan housing carry a heavy grease load that throttles airflow and lowers extract efficiency. A fire in that concealed ductwork spreads fast. That is why a dated TR19 certificate, kept in the fire logbook, is the document an insurer expects to see - and its absence at the point of a claim is a common reason cover is refused.

Planning Your Visit

The town centre is compact and walkable from Dartford station, which sits on the fast line from London Bridge (under 30 minutes) and Cannon Street. Spital Street, Copperfields and the High Street are a five-minute walk from the station, putting Ossie's Cafe, Cadiz Tapas and the Saturday market all within easy reach on foot. The Malt Shovel on Darenth Road is about ten minutes' walk from the station, and Britannia Grill on Dartford Road is a short eastward walk from the centre.

A day in Dartford works well as a half-day from London or an evening out: coffee and breakfast at Ossie's, a walk through the market if it is a Saturday, lunch or dinner at Cadiz Tapas with a bottle of Spanish wine, and a pint at the Malt Shovel to finish. For a lighter visit, Britannia Grill wraps up an evening efficiently. The Thames crossing and the Bluewater retail park are both close if you want to extend the day into the wider area.

  • Arrive at Ossie's Cafe on Copperfields for a full English from 7:30am Monday to Saturday
  • Visit the Saturday High Street market or the third-Friday farmers' market for Kent produce
  • Book a table at Cadiz Tapas on Spital Street; try the paella or pork cheek and a cocktail
  • Pick up a kebab from Britannia Grill on Dartford Road, open seven evenings a week
  • Finish at the Malt Shovel on Darenth Road for a pint of Youngs and the 1880s brewery mirror

What to Order and How to Do the Day

Order with intent. At Britannia Grill, the chicken shish wrap or the mixed grill: fresh from the charcoal, generous, and no-nonsense. At Ossie's Cafe, the full English is the reason to go, but the homemade milkshakes and the daily curry special are worth knowing about. At Cadiz Tapas, ask chef Maria what she is charcoal-grilling that evening and order the paella to share; the pork cheek is the single-plate recommendation if you are eating alone. At The Malt Shovel, ask the bar staff which guest ale just came on and take it to the low-ceilinged tap room to look at the Dartford Brewery mirror.

Frequently Asked Questions

Dartford Food and Drink FAQ

Where is the best takeaway in Dartford?

Britannia Grill at 139 Dartford Road is the long-standing local favourite, independently run since 2001 and consistently praised as the best kebab in the town. The chicken shish wrap and mixed grill are the orders to make. Open seven evenings, collection only, no delivery.

Where can I get a good breakfast or cafe meal in Dartford?

Ossie's Cafe at Unit 10, Copperfields, off Spital Street, is Dartford's best-loved independent cafe, open Monday to Saturday from 7:30am. Run by the owner and his family, it serves a celebrated full English alongside homemade milkshakes and daily lunch specials. Over 2,800 Tripadvisor reviews at 4.5 stars.

Which is the best independent restaurant in Dartford?

Cadiz Tapas Restaurant and Cocktail Bar at 70 Spital Street serves authentic Spanish tapas and charcoal-grilled dishes under head chef Maria, who trained in Spain for over two decades. Owner Lorenzo runs the room with close attention; the paella is the standout. Book ahead for weekends through the restaurant website or OpenTable.

Where is the best pub in Dartford for real ale?

The Malt Shovel at 3 Darenth Road dates from 1673 and has been in the CAMRA Good Beer Guide every year since 2009. It serves Young's Original, St Austell Tribute and rotating guest ales in a tap room with an original 1880s Dartford Brewery mirror. Monday quiz, Tuesday crib and a monthly jazz jam.

What is Dartford known for as a town?

Dartford is a historic north-Kent market town at the Darent river crossing, with medieval roots and a Saturday market that still runs on the High Street. It is also the birthplace of Mick Jagger and the place where he and Keith Richards met in 1961. The town is close to the Dartford Crossing and the Bluewater shopping centre, but its independent food scene is centred on Spital Street and the Copperfields quarter.

Is Dartford worth visiting for a food day out?

Yes, especially if you combine the Saturday market with lunch at Cadiz Tapas and an evening pint at the Malt Shovel. The town is under 30 minutes from London Bridge by train, making it practical for a half-day visit. Ossie's Cafe is a strong breakfast start, and Britannia Grill rounds off an evening well. Most venues sit within a ten-minute walk of Dartford station.
