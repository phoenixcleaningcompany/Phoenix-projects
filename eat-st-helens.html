Where to Eat in St Helens

Quick Answer

For a fast answer: Tanan's Fish and Chips on Westfield Street for freshly battered cod with mushy peas, Toast Coffee House on Dentons Green Lane for a home-cooked breakfast in a cobbled courtyard, La Casa Vieja on Bickerstaffe Street for authentic Spanish tapas from a Gran Canaria-born chef, and The Cricketers Arms on Peter Street for cask ales from the on-site Howzat brewery. Four moods, one town.

Rather than list everywhere in St Helens, this guide names four places worth going out of your way for, one in each everyday category, and lets them point you towards the rest of the town.

The Four Picks

Takeaway

Tanan's Fish and Chips

27 Westfield Street, town centre

Tanan's is a proper sit-down fish and chip restaurant in the town centre, not a hatch-and-walk-away chippy. Tiled walls give it a vintage diner feel, table service comes with a warm welcome, and the kitchen cooks to order rather than holding fish under a lamp. The cod arrives crisp, fresh and generous, paired with hearty chips and mushy peas, and the strong cup of tea that follows is non-negotiable. A food safety inspection in March 2026 rated the premises five stars, the top score, confirming the kitchen matches the reputation. Monday to Saturday lunchtimes, 11:30 to 2:30, with dine-in and takeaway both catered for.

Cafe

Toast Coffee House

149 Dentons Green Lane, Dentons Green

Toast has been a firm fixture in the Dentons Green neighbourhood since 2011, rated one of the top cafes in St Helens and holding a 4.7-star Google average across hundreds of reviews. The setting is part of its charm: inside, the main room is warm and unpretentious; outside, a covered and heated cobbled courtyard makes it one of the few dog-friendly cafes in the borough. Breakfasts are cooked fresh to order and the salt-and-pepper distinction is earned rather than claimed. Staff are consistently praised, the full-English holds up to scrutiny, and afternoon tea can be arranged for something more leisurely. Open from 9am to 4pm, Tuesday to Sunday.

Restaurant

La Casa Vieja

12 Bickerstaffe Street, town centre (near St Helens Central station)

La Casa Vieja has been feeding St Helens for over 30 years from a courtyard just off Bickerstaffe Street, a short walk from St Helens Central station. Head chef Manolo Estupinan-Quintana, who grew up in Gran Canaria, and his wife Joan opened the restaurant after meeting in the Canary Islands and moving to Merseyside to raise their family. Their children Siobhan and Mikel now work alongside them. The menu runs to wafer-thin jamon serrano, Spanish black pudding, octopus in red wine, and a fish soup that regulars travel for. The accolades are kept current: a TripAdvisor Travelers Choice award for 2025 and a consistent rating that puts it in the top handful of restaurants in the borough. Evenings only; book ahead.

Pub

The Cricketers Arms

64 Peter Street, town centre

The Cricketers Arms is the standard-bearer for St Helens real-ale culture: a family-run community pub on Peter Street with 14 hand-pulls, house cider and a beer range that rotates constantly. In 2020 the team installed the on-site Howzat Brewery, producing their own house ales alongside guest casks from independent breweries across the country. The pub was named CAMRA National Pub of the Year in 2017 and was awarded St Helens and Districts CAMRA Pub of the Year again in 2025, confirming a reputation that has only grown. Food is honest and well-matched to the beer: stone-baked pizzas from a dedicated pizza kitchen (Thursday to Sunday), cheese boards and hotpot. Dogs, dart boards, a beer garden and live sport complete the picture.

St Helens's Food Scene

St Helens is a working-class Merseyside town shaped by two industries: glassmaking, which Pilkington built into a world operation from 1826, and rugby league, where the Saints have been the most successful Super League club of the modern era. Both leave their mark on the eating culture: practical, unpretentious, rooted in real neighbourhoods rather than a gastro-quarter imported from elsewhere.

The town centre clusters around Church Street and the market, with the pubs of Peter Street and Cooper Street forming one of the most celebrated real-ale trails outside a major city. St Helens and Districts CAMRA has long recognised the quality here, and the Cricketers Arms, Turks Head and Cowley Vaults between them offer a range of cask ales that would embarrass many larger towns. Independent cafes serve the breakfast and lunch trade, and a handful of long-running restaurants have built loyal followings over decades.

The standout of that longer-running generation is La Casa Vieja, a Spanish tapas kitchen that has traded in a courtyard off Bickerstaffe Street for over 30 years, quietly earning a reputation as one of the most authentic tapas rooms in the north-west. Its survival and popularity says something about the town: St Helens does not chase food trends, but when it finds something worth keeping, it keeps it.

Behind the Pass: The Kitchens That Keep St Helens Fed

Behind every bar in this guide is a kitchen, and behind every kitchen an extraction system - canopy, baffle filters, plenum, ductwork and fan - drawing grease-laden air off the cookline and out through the roof. St Helens is pub-proud territory: its town-centre pubs run multiple hand-pulls and the Cricketers Arms even brews its own house ales on site, meaning short, busy service sessions generating a high, continuous load of grease-laden vapour into every canopy.

Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the "responsible person" for a premises must assess and manage fire risk, and a grease-laden duct is one of the most serious risks in any catering building. The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) reinforce the same duty of care, while an Environmental Health Officer (EHO) or a buildings insurer can ask to see documented proof the extraction system has been professionally cleaned.

What is TR19 Grease?

TR19 Grease is the UK industry standard for cleaning ductwork and extraction systems in commercial kitchens, published by the Building Engineering Services Association (BESA). It defines how grease deposits are measured, the maximum thickness allowed before cleaning is due, and the post-clean certification a kitchen should hold - the benchmark insurers, fire assessors and EHOs look for.

A great pub kitchen lives or dies on its food. The extraction system is what keeps it open, insured and safe behind the scenes.

Industry Insight

The Cleaning Frequency Most Operators Get Wrong

Here is something many venue owners do not realise: under TR19 Grease, cleaning frequency is set by how many hours the kitchen actually cooks, not by how the canopy looks. A heavy-use kitchen running 12 to 16 hours a day should be cleaned roughly every three months. Moderate use of 6 to 12 hours points to every six months, and light use of 2 to 6 hours to every twelve.

The catch is that baffle filters can look clean while the plenum, the horizontal duct runs and the fan housing carry a heavy grease load that throttles airflow and lowers extract efficiency. A fire in that concealed ductwork spreads fast. That is why a dated TR19 certificate, kept in the fire logbook, is the document an insurer expects to see - and its absence at the point of a claim is a common reason cover is refused.

Planning Your Visit

The four picks sit within easy walking distance of each other in or near the town centre. St Helens Central and St Helens Junction stations both drop you into the middle of things, and the Peter Street pubs are a five-minute walk from either. Tanan's on Westfield Street is a short walk south, and La Casa Vieja is practically next door to St Helens Central.

The natural order is: Toast for breakfast in Dentons Green (a short drive or bus ride north of the centre), Tanan's for a lunchtime fish and chips, a browse around the World of Glass museum in the afternoon, then La Casa Vieja for a tapas evening and The Cricketers Arms for a post-dinner pint from the Howzat taps. Book La Casa Vieja ahead; it fills quickly and does not take walk-ins easily.

  • Book La Casa Vieja well in advance - it is evenings-only and fills quickly
  • Arrive at Tanan's before the lunchtime rush, Monday to Saturday 11:30-2:30
  • Visit the World of Glass museum to understand the Pilkington heritage
  • Call at the Cricketers Arms on a Thursday to Sunday for the pizza kitchen
  • Toast Coffee House is Tuesday to Sunday from 9am; the courtyard is dog-friendly

What to Order and How to Do the Day

Order with intent. At Tanan's, the freshly battered cod with chips, mushy peas and a pot of tea - the classics, done properly. At Toast, a full English cooked to order, or ask what is on for brunch that day. At La Casa Vieja, work through the tapas: the Spanish black pudding, jamon serrano, and octopus in red wine are the picks, with the fish soup if it is on. At the Cricketers Arms, ask the bar which Howzat house ale is freshest and let them guide you to the rotating cask.

Frequently Asked Questions

St Helens Food and Drink FAQ

Where can I get the best fish and chips in St Helens?

Tanan's Fish and Chips on Westfield Street is widely rated the best chippy in the town centre, offering freshly battered cod with table service in a tiled diner setting, with a five-star food hygiene rating awarded in March 2026.

Is there a good independent cafe in St Helens for breakfast?

Toast Coffee House on Dentons Green Lane has been one of the borough's best-loved cafes since 2011. It serves home-cooked breakfasts from 9am, has a covered and heated cobbled courtyard, and is dog-friendly.

What is the best restaurant in St Helens for a special occasion?

La Casa Vieja on Bickerstaffe Street, a family-run Spanish tapas restaurant open for over 30 years, is consistently rated among the top restaurants in the borough and holds a TripAdvisor Travelers Choice award for 2025. Book ahead as it fills quickly.

Which pub in St Helens has the best real ale?

The Cricketers Arms on Peter Street was named St Helens and Districts CAMRA Pub of the Year in 2025 and CAMRA National Pub of the Year in 2017. It has 14 hand-pulls and brews its own house ales through the on-site Howzat Brewery, which has been producing beer since 2020.

What is St Helens famous for besides rugby league?

St Helens is the global home of flat glass. Pilkington, founded here in 1826, invented the float glass process in 1959 and grew to become one of the world's leading glass manufacturers. The World of Glass museum in the town centre tells that story, with live glassblowing demonstrations and exhibits covering the industry from ancient Egypt to the modern day.

Can you do a food day in St Helens on foot?

Easily, for most of it. Tanan's, La Casa Vieja and The Cricketers Arms are all within ten minutes walk of St Helens Central station. Toast Coffee House is in the Dentons Green neighbourhood to the north, a short bus ride or drive from the centre.
