The honest answer on scale

Three layers get you to tens of thousands — safely

Tens of thousands of unique editorial pages isn't realistic without repeating yourself. But a large, safe footprint absolutely is — it just comes from three different layers, and most of the volume lives in the catalogue, not the content.

Layer 1 · Content site
~1,350

Genuinely unique guides

The 14 families below. Grows to ~2,000–3,000 with editorial over 2–3 years. This is the authority engine — every page passes the gate.

Layer 2 · Commerce (Shopify)
1,000s

Product & collection pages

Where the real volume lives. Each SKU is unique by actual spec. Content links point into these — many-to-few, not one-to-one.

Layer 3 · Local
Only if real

Data-backed local pages

Legitimate only with real local data (stockists, depots, delivery). Near-zero now — the retired town‑swap trap. Do not fake it.

Where the counts come from

Four real data axes

Page counts aren't guesses — they're the number of real entities worth a dedicated page in each axis. Transparent and defensible.

The discipline that keeps it safe: the sector × role matrix is deliberately not fully crossed — 38 × 180 would be 6,840 near-duplicates. Only ~300 genuinely differentiated combinations are built (where the hazards and standards actually differ). Everything else stays a section within a parent page, not a standalone URL.
