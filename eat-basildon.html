Where to Eat in Basildon

Quick Answer

For a fast answer: Wok and Roll in Pitsea for Cantonese cooking that has been running since 2003, Cosy Cafe on Southernhay for the best full breakfast in the town centre, The Magic Mushroom at Barleylands for Darren Bennett's two-AA-Rosette British and Mediterranean cooking, and The Shepherd and Dog in Crays Hill for chef Rebecca Bailey's gastropub between Billericay and Wickford. Four very different moods, one Essex new town.

There is far more to eat in Basildon than any one page could hold, so this guide keeps it honest: one real, independent standout per category - somewhere to grab a quick bite, a proper coffee, a meal out and a pint - chosen on merit.

The Four Picks

Takeaway

Wok and Roll

Repton Court, Pitsea

Pitsea is the eastern quarter of the Basildon borough, home to one of Essex's oldest open-air markets and a quietly loyal restaurant strip. Wok and Roll has traded from Repton Court in Pitsea since the business was incorporated in September 2003, building a reputation for consistent Cantonese and Pekinese cooking across more than two decades. The menu covers the full range from crispy aromatic duck and salt-and-pepper king prawns to classic chicken chow mein and BBQ spare ribs, with generous portions that regulars say justify the trip across town. It operates as a pure takeaway, open evenings Tuesday through Sunday, and a December 2025 food hygiene inspection confirms it is actively trading. For a no-frills, long-running independent that knows exactly what it is doing, this is the Basildon pick.

Cafe

Cosy Cafe

57 Southernhay, Basildon town centre

Southernhay is the commercial strip that connects Basildon station to the town centre market, and Cosy Cafe at number 57 has become the locals' answer to where to eat breakfast in town. The independent cafe serves a full English that draws repeat visits for its generous portions and freshly cooked ingredients, alongside jacket potatoes, sandwiches, burgers, and omelettes through the day. The room is unpretentious and friendly, with quick service and prices that reflect a neighbourhood cafe rather than a branded chain. It was inspected by the Food Standards Agency in April 2025 and updated as open on Yelp in July 2025, and local reviewers consistently rate it the best cafe in Basildon for a straightforward cooked breakfast.

Restaurant

The Magic Mushroom

Barleylands Road, Billericay

Set on the edge of a working farm at Barleylands, just outside Billericay in the southern half of the Basildon borough, The Magic Mushroom is the area's landmark special-occasion table. Chef-owner Darren Bennett opened the restaurant in 1996 and has run it ever since, cooking British and Mediterranean menus built on locally sourced and organic produce from the surrounding Essex farmland. In March 2026 the AA awarded the kitchen its second Rosette, a nationally recognised mark of culinary excellence that puts it firmly in the top tier of Essex restaurants. The dining room is elegant without being stuffy, the service warm, and the setting on a farm village site means the experience feels entirely unlike eating in a town-centre restaurant. Open Wednesday to Sunday; book ahead for weekends.

Pub

The Shepherd and Dog

London Road, Crays Hill (between Billericay and Wickford)

Crays Hill is a village on the London Road that sits almost exactly between Billericay and Wickford, and The Shepherd and Dog is its local pub transformed. Chef and owner Rebecca Bailey trained at Leith's School of Food and Wine in London and spent years in private catering before taking over the pub in October 2019 with her daughter, refitting it as a Great British gastropub within weeks. In the kitchen alongside her is head chef Carlo Mason, with over 36 years of experience including training in Michelin-starred restaurants. The menu is built around fresh, locally sourced ingredients: the steak, ale and horseradish sauce pie is the house signature, with sticky toffee pudding to finish. TripAdvisor rated it a 2025 Travellers' Choice award, placing it in the top 10 per cent of restaurants globally. A real fire in winter and a family garden in summer.

Basildon's Food Scene

Basildon is a post-war new town, designated in 1949 to absorb London's overspill, and its food landscape reflects that history. The borough stitches together old villages and new housing estates: Pitsea to the east, with one of Essex's oldest open-air markets dating to around 1925; Laindon to the west; and the town centre itself, built from scratch across the 1950s and 1960s. Takeaways, cafes, and curry houses serve a dense residential population, and the Festival Leisure Park off the ring road brings a cluster of restaurants under one roof.

The more interesting eating is out in the borough's older settled areas. Billericay, a few miles north, has a High Street and a farming hinterland, and it is here that the borough's finest restaurant sits: The Magic Mushroom at Barleylands farm, a two-AA-Rosette kitchen that has been drawing diners from across south Essex since 1996. Billericay also has its own microbrewery taproom, the Billericay Brewing Company, founded in 2012 by Trevor Jeffery and active on the local real ale circuit.

In the village of Crays Hill, between Billericay and Wickford, The Shepherd and Dog shows what a Leith-trained chef can do with an old country pub. Meanwhile Pitsea's Chinese takeaway strip speaks to the borough's multicultural makeup and its appetite for long-established neighbourhood cooking. Pitsea Market itself runs Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday and includes food stalls alongside its general traders.

Behind the Pass: The Kitchens That Keep Basildon Fed

Every venue in this guide runs on a commercial kitchen, and behind each cookline sits an extraction system - canopy, baffle filters, plenum, ductwork and roof fan - pulling heat, smoke and grease-laden vapour out of the building. Basildon is a dense, working new town with a high-volume food trade: its takeaways, town-centre cafes, and the commercial kitchens at Festival Leisure Park run long shifts that pour grease-laden vapour into extraction canopies every session.

Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the "responsible person" for a premises must assess and manage fire risk, and a grease-laden duct is one of the most serious risks in any catering building. The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) reinforce the same duty of care, while an Environmental Health Officer (EHO) or a buildings insurer can ask to see documented proof the extraction system has been professionally cleaned.

What is TR19 Grease?

TR19 Grease is the UK industry standard for cleaning ductwork and extraction systems in commercial kitchens, published by the Building Engineering Services Association (BESA). It defines how grease deposits are measured, the maximum thickness allowed before cleaning is due, and the post-clean certification a kitchen should hold - the benchmark insurers, fire assessors and EHOs look for.

The food gets the headlines. The extraction system is what keeps the kitchen open, insured and safe - quietly, behind the pass.

Industry Insight

The Cleaning Frequency Most Operators Get Wrong

Here is something many venue owners do not realise: under TR19 Grease, cleaning frequency is set by how many hours the kitchen actually cooks, not by how the canopy looks. A heavy-use kitchen running 12 to 16 hours a day should be cleaned roughly every three months. Moderate use of 6 to 12 hours points to every six months, and light use of 2 to 6 hours to every twelve.

The catch is that baffle filters can look clean while the plenum, the horizontal duct runs and the fan housing carry a heavy grease load that throttles airflow and lowers extract efficiency. A fire in that concealed ductwork spreads fast. That is why a dated TR19 certificate, kept in the fire logbook, is the document an insurer expects to see - and its absence at the point of a claim is a common reason cover is refused.

Planning Your Visit

These four places spread across the borough, so a car helps. Cosy Cafe on Southernhay is a two-minute walk from Basildon station and makes a natural start. Wok and Roll in Pitsea is about three miles east; Pitsea station is on the same c2c line. The Magic Mushroom at Barleylands and The Shepherd and Dog in Crays Hill are both a short drive north towards Billericay.

Time it to do Pitsea Market first on a Saturday or Sunday morning (stalls from 9am), then lunch at Cosy Cafe in town. In the evening, The Shepherd and Dog is well worth the drive out to Crays Hill, and The Magic Mushroom is the area's best option for a special occasion dinner: book well ahead for a Friday or Saturday.

  • Start the day at Cosy Cafe on Southernhay for one of the best full Englishes in Basildon
  • Visit Pitsea Market on a Wednesday, Friday, Saturday or Sunday morning for local traders and food stalls
  • Order the duck or spare ribs at Wok and Roll in Pitsea - an Essex Chinese institution since 2003
  • Book ahead for The Magic Mushroom at Barleylands - it is the two-AA-Rosette destination for the whole of south Essex
  • Try the steak, ale and horseradish pie at The Shepherd and Dog in Crays Hill, then stay for pudding

What to Order and How to Do the Day

At Wok and Roll, the crispy aromatic duck is the classic order, with salt-and-pepper king prawns if you want something lighter; the BBQ spare ribs are a Pitsea staple. At Cosy Cafe, the full English with everything, or a jacket potato if you have already eaten once that day. At The Magic Mushroom, follow the set menu and trust the chef with locally sourced Essex produce. At The Shepherd and Dog, the steak, ale and horseradish sauce pie is the signature; finish with sticky toffee pudding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Basildon Food and Drink FAQ

Where is the best place for breakfast in Basildon?

Cosy Cafe at 57 Southernhay in the town centre is consistently rated the best cafe in Basildon for a full English breakfast. It is an independent, freshly cooked and priced for a local audience, a two-minute walk from Basildon station.

Is there a fine-dining restaurant near Basildon?

The Magic Mushroom at Barleylands farm on Barleylands Road, Billericay, is the borough's destination restaurant. Chef-owner Darren Bennett has run it since 1996 and it holds two AA Rosettes as of March 2026, the highest-rated independent kitchen in south Essex.

What is the best Chinese takeaway in Basildon?

Wok and Roll on Repton Court in Pitsea has been serving Cantonese and Pekinese cooking since 2003 and is regarded as one of the most consistent Chinese takeaways in the borough. Open evenings Tuesday to Sunday.

Is there a gastropub near Basildon?

The Shepherd and Dog on London Road in Crays Hill, between Billericay and Wickford, is owned and run by Rebecca Bailey, who trained at Leith's School of Food and Wine. It holds a TripAdvisor 2025 Travellers' Choice award and is the area's best gastropub, with a steak, ale and horseradish sauce pie as the signature dish.

Does Basildon have a market?

Pitsea Market, in the eastern part of the borough, dates to around 1925 and is one of the oldest open-air markets in Essex. It trades Wednesday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday from 9am to 3.30pm (Friday until 2.30pm), with food stalls alongside clothing and general goods.

How do I get around the Basildon food places by public transport?

Cosy Cafe in the town centre and Wok and Roll in Pitsea are both near c2c stations on the London Fenchurch Street line. The Magic Mushroom at Barleylands and The Shepherd and Dog in Crays Hill are easier to reach by car; Billericay station is about two miles from Barleylands.
