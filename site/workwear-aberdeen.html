What Aberdeen works in

An energy economy, and a harbour

Aberdeen is the UK's offshore capital. The city and its heliport at Dyce run the North Sea's oil & gas, subsea engineering and, increasingly, decommissioning and offshore wind — the Energy Transition Zone is pulling the same trades into renewables. Around it sits one of Scotland's busiest fishing and seafood-processing harbours, and the wide agriculture of Aberdeenshire beyond the ring road.

That mix is why Aberdeen workwear is unusual. An offshore or energy job carries flame, hydrocarbon-static and low-light hazards at the same time, so the region specifies multi-norm garments that a general mainland trade never would.

Aberdeen workwear FAQ

What workwear do offshore workers in Aberdeen need?

Most Aberdeen offshore operators specify a multi-norm coverall combining flame resistance (EN ISO 11612), antistatic protection (EN 1149-5) and high-visibility (EN ISO 20471) in one garment, because a North Sea installation carries hydrocarbon, spark and low-light risks together. Hot work adds EN ISO 11611, chemical handling adds EN 13034, and deck work in winter adds EN 342 cold and wind protection. Always check the operator's own PPE matrix — it sets the exact combination.

Do you deliver workwear to Aberdeen and Aberdeenshire?

Yes. iNeedWorkwear is a nationwide supplier with next-working-day delivery to AB postcodes — Aberdeen, Dyce, Altens, Bridge of Don and the wider Aberdeenshire towns. We're not a shop in the city; we supply and ship, with trade accounts and logo embroidery available.

Why do Aberdeen energy jobs require antistatic clothing?

Because much of the energy estate is a potentially explosive atmosphere — hydrocarbons and flammable vapours are present. A static discharge from ordinary clothing can be an ignition source, so EN 1149-5 antistatic fabric is specified alongside flame resistance. It's one of the defining differences between Aberdeen energy workwear and general trade kit.
