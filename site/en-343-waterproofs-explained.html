Standard explainer · EN 343

EN 343: what "waterproof" actually promises.

"Waterproof" on a work jacket is a certified claim with two numbers behind it: how hard the fabric resists rain getting in, and how well it lets sweat get out. Most buying mistakes come from reading only the first one.

The two numbers

Keeping rain out is half the job

EN 343 rates protective clothing against rain with two classes, shown together on the garment pictogram:

  • X — resistance to water penetration (class 1–4): how much water pressure the fabric and seams withstand before leaking. Class 4, added in the current revision, is tested after pre-treatment that simulates wear.
  • Y — water vapour resistance (class 1–4): breathability. Higher class = sweat vapour escapes more easily.

The second number is where comfort lives. A worker generating heat inside a non-breathable shell gets wet from their own sweat as surely as from rain — colder, clammier and slower to dry. That's the 4:1 trap: maximum penetration resistance, minimum breathability, miserable by mid-morning if the work is physical.

Keeping it rated

EN 343 performance is maintained, not just bought

The certified performance assumes the garment's DWR coating is alive and its seams intact. When rain stops beading and the face fabric wets out, breathability collapses and the jacket "leaks" without leaking — the fix is cleaning and reproofing, covered step-by-step in reproofing waterproofs.

On hi-vis waterproofs, remember the garment is certified twice over — the rain rating and EN ISO 20471 visibility — and each wash counts against the hi-vis wash limit. Buy once, maintain deliberately.

Questions

EN 343 FAQ

What do the EN 343 numbers mean?

Two classes, each 1–4: the first is resistance to water penetration (how hard rain is kept out), the second is water vapour resistance (breathability — how easily sweat escapes). Higher is better in both; the current revision added class 4 penetration, tested after simulated wear.

Is a class 4 EN 343 jacket the best?

Only if the breathability class is healthy too. A 4:1 garment keeps rain out and sweat in — fine for static roles, miserable for physical work. For high-output outdoor trades, aim for 3 or 4 in both positions.

Why does my EN 343 jacket feel like it leaks?

Usually it's wetting out, not leaking: the DWR coating has worn off, the face fabric soaks up water, and breathability collapses — so sweat condenses inside. Washing the jacket clean and applying a wash-in reproofer restores it; genuine leaks track to seams or damage.

Does EN 343 mean the jacket is warm?

No — EN 343 covers rain only. Cold protection is a separate standard (EN 342 for below −5 °C environments), which is why winter site jackets are often certified to both, or worn as a shell over insulating layers.
