Drying workwear: the overnight problem.

The hardest workwear problem in Britain isn't washing — it's Tuesday night: soaked kit, a 6am start, and a tumble dryer that ruins half of what goes in it. Drying is where good garments die young and where damp ones quietly become a health issue, so it deserves the ten minutes of system the trade rarely gives it.

The rules

What tumbles, what doesn't

The dryer's casualty list is long and specific: waterproof membranes and taped seams delaminate (unless the label explicitly allows low tumble — some DWR revival actually wants it, label first); elastane-rich stretch fabrics lose their snap; prints and transfers crack; hi-vis fades faster (the compliance angle); FR garments follow their label exactly (the FR rules); and anything still stained gets its stain heat-set permanently. What tumbles happily: cotton-rich tees, sweats, polycotton trousers without stretch, towelling. The label knows; the habit doesn't ask.

The overnight system that works without a dryer is a drying room shrunk to a corner: garments spaced on hangers (not heaped on a radiator), moving air from a cheap fan doing more than heat ever does, modest background warmth, and ideally a dehumidifier — which dries kit faster than a radiator and doesn't wallpaper the room with condensation. Boots get their own rule: insoles out, newspaper or a boot dryer, never the radiator top — direct heat cracks leather and delaminates soles, which is how wet boots become dead boots in one winter.

Questions

Drying FAQ

Can you tumble dry work trousers?

Plain polycotton trousers usually yes, on the label's setting — but stretch (elastane) content, waterproof membranes and knee-pad pocket linings often say no or low. Check each garment's label once and sort loads accordingly; one hot load ruins stretch recovery permanently.

How do you dry workwear overnight without a dryer?

Space garments on hangers, add moving air (a basic fan) and modest warmth, ideally with a dehumidifier — airflow dries faster than radiator heat and doesn't condense on the windows. Heaped-on-radiator drying is slow and cooks the contact points.

How do you dry wet work boots?

Insoles out, laces loosened, stuffed with newspaper (changed once) or on a boot dryer — never on or against a radiator, which cracks leather and delaminates soles. Overnight is usually enough with airflow; two pairs in rotation is the winter answer.
