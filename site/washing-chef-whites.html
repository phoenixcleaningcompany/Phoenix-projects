The routine

Speed beats chemistry

Every laundry product on the shelf loses to one habit: treating the stain the same day. Kitchen soils set with time and heat — a fat splash pre-treated after service shifts easily; the same mark after a hot wash and tumble is dyed in. So the routine is simple: scrape, pre-treat by stain type, hot bio wash, and never heat-dry a garment that's still marked (heat sets what the wash missed — the same rule as workshop oil).

Fabric matters too: cotton-rich whites tolerate the hottest washes and respond to sunlight; poly-cotton dries faster but yellows under chlorine bleach — which is why oxygen-based whiteners are the professional default.

Questions

Whites FAQ

What temperature should chef whites be washed at?

The hottest the care label allows — typically 60 °C or above for cotton-rich whites — with a biological detergent. Hot washing is what keeps hygiene-grade whiteness achievable; the label, not habit, sets the ceiling.

How do you get grease out of chef whites?

Pre-treat the same day with a degreaser or neat washing-up liquid, leave it 20 minutes, then hot wash. Never cold-wash a fresh grease mark first and never tumble-dry a garment still marked — both set the stain.

Can you use bleach on chef whites?

Oxygen-based whiteners, yes — they lift greying safely. Chlorine bleach is the wrong tool: it yellows poly-cotton, weakens fibres, and most whites' care labels prohibit it outright.

Why do chef whites go grey?

Accumulated soil and detergent residue from washes that were too cool, too short or overloaded — plus fabric fatigue. Regular hot bio washes with occasional oxygen-bleach soaks prevent it; once the base tone is grey despite the full routine, the garment is simply tired.
