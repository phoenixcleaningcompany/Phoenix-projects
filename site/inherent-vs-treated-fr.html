Inherent vs treated FR fabric.

Two FR garments can carry identical certifications and be completely different products underneath: one woven from fibres that can't burn, the other from cotton chemically finished to resist it. Both pass the same tests on day one — the difference is what happens over two hundred washes.

The two routes

Chemistry in the fibre, or chemistry on the cotton

Inherent FR fabrics are spun from fibres whose molecular structure doesn't sustain flame — aramids (the Nomex family), modacrylics and blends. The protection is the fibre: it can't wash out, wear off or be laundered away, and it persists for the structural life of the garment.

Treated FR starts as cotton or poly-cotton and gains its resistance from a flame-retardant finish bonded into the fabric (Proban-type processes and successors). Done properly it's genuinely certified protection with cotton's comfort and a friendlier price — but the finish is a consumable: it survives a specified number of correct wash cycles, and incorrect laundering (bleach, softener, wrong chemistry) depletes it early, invisibly.

Choosing

Decide on laundering reality, not brochure life

The right question isn't "which fabric is better" — it's "what will actually happen to this garment in the wash?" A fleet laundered industrially, on contract, to the garment maker's spec keeps treated FR inside its rated life comfortably — and pockets the price difference. Kit washed at home, by many hands, with whatever's next to the machine, is exactly where treated finishes die early — and where inherent fabric's tolerance for abuse pays for itself.

Two universal caveats: both types lose protection to hydrocarbon contamination (oil is fuel), and both follow the same laundering rules (no softener, no bleach) — inherent fabric survives rule-breaking better, but softener film makes any fabric's surface flammable.

Questions

Inherent vs treated FAQ

What is the difference between inherent and treated FR?

Inherent FR fabric is woven from fibres that are flame-resistant by chemistry (aramids, modacrylics) — the protection can't wash out. Treated FR is cotton finished with a flame-retardant treatment — certified protection with a specified wash life that incorrect laundering shortens.

Is inherent FR better than treated?

It's more durable, not more protective — on day one both certify to the same standards. Inherent wins where garments live long and laundering is uncontrolled; treated wins on price and cotton comfort where wash discipline is good. Cost per wear, not the label, is the fair comparison.

Can treated FR wash out?

The finish is rated for a stated number of correct wash cycles and depletes early under bleach, softener or wrong wash chemistry — without changing how the garment looks. That invisible failure mode is why treated fleets need laundering discipline and cycle awareness.

Do inherent FR garments last forever?

The flame resistance persists, but the garment doesn't — abrasion, rips, UV and contamination still retire it. And a softener film or oil soaking makes any FR fabric's surface ignitable, inherent included. Care rules apply to both types equally.
