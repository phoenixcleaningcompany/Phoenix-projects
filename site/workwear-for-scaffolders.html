Role guide · Scaffolding

What scaffolders actually wear.

Scaffolding is climbing with cargo: tube, boards and fittings moved by hand, at height, in whatever the sky is doing, with a harness over everything. The kit list is written by three facts — everything must work under a harness, everything must move like sportswear, and hands are the tools.

The job

Harness first, wardrobe second

Everything a scaffolder wears interacts with the harness. Bulky jackets bunch under leg loops and chest straps; loose hoods foul the dorsal attachment; slippery shell fabrics let straps migrate. The working answer is slim, stretchy layers — fitted softshells and fleeces rather than padded bulk, with the waterproof shell cut to sit under or over the harness per the firm's policy (the harness itself, and its inspection, is employer-issued fall-protection territory, not a workwear purchase).

Trousers are the signature item: four-way stretch, holster pockets that close (loose fittings fall — see the FOD logic every trade at height shares), reinforced knees, and cut high enough at the back that climbing doesn't expose skin to a December wind. Scaffolders burn through trousers like consumables; buy the trousers guide's durable end.

Questions

Scaffolder FAQ

What trousers do scaffolders wear?

Stretch work trousers with closing holster pockets, reinforced knees and a high back rise — effectively climbing trousers that carry fittings. Durability matters (scaffolding eats fabric on tube and boards), and pockets must close so nothing falls from height.

What gloves are best for scaffolding?

Grip-palmed gloves with genuine cut resistance (EN 388 cut level B or better) and enough dexterity for fittings work — with a thermal EN 511-rated version for winter, because cold galvanised steel drains hands fast. Scaffolders go through gloves quickly; buy by the box.

Does workwear need to be harness-compatible?

Yes in practice: slim fitted layers that don't bunch under leg loops or chest straps, no loose hoods around the dorsal point, and shells worn per the firm's harness policy. The harness and fall-arrest kit itself is employer-issued and inspected equipment, separate from the workwear order.
