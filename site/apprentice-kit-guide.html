Apprentice kit: day one, dressed right.

The legal split

What you buy vs what they provide

Clear the biggest confusion first: PPE that a risk assessment requires must be provided free by the employer — hard hats, required hi-vis, task gloves, eye and hearing protection, FR, and in most cases safety footwear where it's mandated. The full position is in who pays for PPE. What apprentices genuinely end up buying is the workwear around it: trousers, tees, mid-layers, and often a first pair of boots before the employer's arrangements kick in.

So before spending: ask the employer or training provider for the kit list. A good one tells you exactly what's issued and what's yours. The list below covers the near-universal personal buys.

Questions

Apprentice FAQ

Do apprentices have to buy their own PPE?

No — PPE required by the risk assessment must be provided free of charge by the employer, apprentice or not. What apprentices commonly do buy is general workwear (trousers, tees, layers) and sometimes a first pair of safety boots before employer arrangements kick in. Ask for the kit list before spending anything.

What safety boots should an apprentice buy?

A well-fitting S1P or S3 boot from a reputable maker — S3 if the work is outdoors in mud, S1P often suffices indoors. Fit beats features: try them on late in the day, walk properly, and spend what you can afford here rather than on logo tees. Your feet do every shift of a forty-year career.

How much does starter workwear cost?

A sensible day-one bundle — boots, two work trousers, four or five tees, a mid-layer, a waterproof and basics — is typically low hundreds of pounds, with boots rightly the biggest single line. Buy the cheap items cheap and the boots good; upgrade everything else as the trade teaches you what it eats.
