The specifics

Wind first, and dress for the harness

Ground-level layering logic underestimates roofs: wind is the multiplier, stripping heat through fleece that would be fine in the yard. The roof answer is windproofing early — softshell over base layer — with the waterproof shell for actual rain, because roof work in real rain usually stops for the same reason the tiles are slippery.

Harness compatibility quietly shapes the wardrobe: bulky insulated jackets bunch under straps and hide the fit check, so roofers run trim layers under the harness and put warmth in the base and mid layers instead. Torch-on and hot works add the flame layer: an EN 407-rated glove, natural-fibre or FR outer near the torch, and the melt-layer rule from the FR hub applying to whatever's underneath.

Questions

Roofer kit FAQ

What boots are best for roofing?

S3 boots with an aggressive slip-rated sole and a flexible forefoot — grip on wet tile, membrane and crawl boards is the primary spec, and a stiff sole fights the footwork. Tread condition gets inspected like the safety equipment it is.

What do roofers wear in winter?

Windproofing before insulation: wicking base layer, windproof softshell, and a waterproof shell for real weather — roof wind doubles the effective cold, and bulky coats fight the harness. Warm gloves that still grip, and a helmet-compatible beanie, finish it.

Do roofers need special helmets?

Work at height favours chinstrap-retained climbing-style helmets (EN 12492) that stay on in a fall or gust, and many roofing contractors now specify them over traditional EN 397 lids. The site or employer's rules make the final call — check before buying.
