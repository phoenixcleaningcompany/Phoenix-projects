The static problem

Standing still is a cold-weather sport

A patrol officer walking generates several times the heat of one posted static — which is why the same jacket that overheats a groundworker leaves a gatehouse officer shivering. The static answer is more insulation than looks reasonable: a longline insulated parka over a mid-layer over a proper base layer, warm footwear with room for circulation (tight boots on cold concrete are a misery machine), and covered head and hands from the first cold hour.

The other half of the brief is looking like authority rather than trades: dark uniform colours, embroidered branding, and a silhouette that reads corporate. Where duties include traffic or car parks, a Class 2 vest goes over the lot — visibility and identity in one garment.

Questions

Security kit FAQ

What do security guards wear in winter?

More than a moving trade needs: base layer, mid layer and a longline insulated parka, with warm loose-fitting footwear, hat and gloves — static posts generate almost no body heat, so the insulation has to do all the work.

Do security officers need safety footwear?

Depends on the site: gatehouse and retail posts usually suit cushioned occupational footwear (comfort first), while industrial sites with vehicle and goods movement often mandate a toe cap — S1 or the site's stated spec. The assignment instructions decide.

Do security staff need hi-vis?

Wherever duties meet vehicles — car parks, gatehouses, traffic marshalling — a Class 2 vest over the uniform is standard. It doubles as instant identification, which is half its value on a busy site.
