The two-boot strategy

S3 for the site, S5 for the hole

No single boot survives groundworks. The S3 leather boot handles the general site day — walking, machine spotting, kerb lines. The moment the work goes into water, wet concrete or serious mud, it's the S5 safety wellington: waterproof by construction, with the same toe cap, midsole and cleated sole (codes explained). Wet concrete specifically is a chemical burn hazard — wellingtons with trousers over the top, not tucked in, is the pour-day setup.

Both pairs die faster in this trade than any other: mud draws the oils out of leather, so the boot-care routine — mud off same day, never radiator-dried — is worth reading twice.

Questions

Groundworker kit FAQ

What boots do groundworkers need?

Two pairs: S3 leather boots for general site work, and S5 safety wellingtons for excavations, mud and concrete pours. Wet concrete is a chemical burn hazard as well as a soaking — wellingtons with waterproof trousers over the top is the pour-day standard.

Why do groundworkers wear Class 3 hi-vis?

The trade lives around moving plant — excavators slewing, tippers reversing, deliveries arriving — often in poor light and weather. Class 3's larger fluorescent and reflective areas fit that exposure, and many groundworks contractors mandate it with hi-vis trousers.

Are knee pads worth it for groundworks?

They're the item veterans most wish they'd worn from day one. Kerb laying, ducting and drainage mean hours kneeling on aggregate; trouser-insert pads or strap-ons prevent the cumulative damage that shows up years later.
