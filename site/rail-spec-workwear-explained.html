Rail-spec workwear: RIS-3279-TOM explained.

"Rail spec" gets stamped on a lot of orange garments. What it refers to is a specific railway industry standard — RIS-3279-TOM — that governs high-visibility clothing for anyone working on or near the line. Here's what it requires, and what to check before you're stood at an induction.

What it is

An industry standard on top of the CE standard

RIS-3279-TOM is a Railway Industry Standard for high-visibility clothing worn by people working on or about the track. It doesn't replace EN ISO 20471 — garments still certify to that — it narrows it for the rail environment. The headline requirements:

  • Orange, not yellow. The background material must be the standard's specified fluorescent orange — chosen for contrast against lineside vegetation and yellow plant.
  • Upper-body visibility at all times. The working assumption trackside is a compliant orange upper-body garment — vest, polo, jacket — worn whenever you're on or near the line.
  • Certified, in date, in condition. A faded or tape-damaged garment fails rail scrutiny exactly as it fails EN ISO 20471 — and rail inductions actually look.

In practice, most rail workers wear more than the minimum: many principal contractors and possessions require Class 3 upper garments and orange hi-vis trousers as standard, so two-piece orange is the safe default for regular trackside work.

Questions

Rail spec FAQ

What is RIS-3279-TOM?

A Railway Industry Standard governing high-visibility clothing for people working on or near the UK railway. It requires fluorescent orange garments certified to EN ISO 20471, worn on the upper body whenever on or about the track — with principal contractors often adding stricter requirements like Class 3 and orange trousers.

Can you wear yellow hi-vis on the railway?

Not on or near the line — rail-spec clothing must be orange. Yellow remains fine for rail-adjacent premises away from the operational railway, but if your work takes you trackside, orange certified kit is the requirement.

Is rail-spec hi-vis a different certification from EN ISO 20471?

No — rail-spec garments are EN ISO 20471-certified garments that additionally meet the rail industry's requirements, chiefly the specified orange colour. Check the label for the EN ISO 20471 marking and class as you would with any hi-vis.

Do I need orange hi-vis trousers for trackside work?

The upper-body orange garment is the baseline, but many principal contractors and possessions mandate orange trousers and Class 3 uppers as site policy. Check your induction or task brief — for regular trackside work, two-piece orange is the practical default.
