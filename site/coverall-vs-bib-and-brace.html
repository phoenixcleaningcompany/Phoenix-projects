Coverall vs bib & brace.

Both solve the same problem — protecting everything above the boot without a gap at the waist — and they solve it differently enough that most trades have a firm allegiance. Here's the split, and the logic behind it.

The shapes

One garment or a garment and a half

The coverall (boilersuit) is total: sleeves to ankles in one zip, nothing rides up, nothing gaps when you reach or crawl. That's why it owns the under-vehicle and spray-adjacent world — mechanics, machine maintenance, anywhere splash and grime come from every direction.

Bib & brace is trousers plus a chest panel on braces: your own shirt or tee up top, full protection below plus the chest. The open back and sides dump heat that a coverall traps, layers change with the seasons without changing the garment, and the bib pocket sits exactly where a rule, pencil and phone want to live. Painters, joiners, agricultural and outdoor trades lean this way.

The verdict

Choose by where the mess comes from

If contamination arrives from above and every side — under vehicles, machine guts, spray mist — the coverall's total envelope wins and no ventilation argument beats it. If the mess is mostly waist-down and in front — kneeling trades, paint, workshop benches — bib & brace protects what actually gets hit and stays wearable through summer.

Whichever side you land, the workshop fit rules travel with you: snug cuffs and nothing loose near anything that spins, and for oil-heavy work, the two-garment rotation with proper laundering.

Questions

Coverall vs bib FAQ

Why do mechanics wear coveralls but painters wear bib and brace?

Direction of mess. Under a vehicle, oil and grime arrive from every angle, so the coverall's total coverage wins. Painting and kneeling trades take mess mostly on the front and legs — the bib covers exactly that while venting heat out the open back.

Is a bib and brace cooler than a coverall?

Noticeably — the open back and sides dump the heat a coverall traps, and you choose your own top layer by season. It's the main reason outdoor and summer trades lean bib and brace.

Do coveralls and bib and brace come in FR versions?

Yes — both cuts exist in flame-resistant fabrics for welding and hot works, certified under the FR standards. The cut choice follows the same coverage logic; the fabric and certification follow the hazard.
