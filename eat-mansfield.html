Where to Eat in Mansfield

Quick Answer

For a fast answer: Andwhynot on Leeming Street for wood-fired pizza and Mediterranean plates next to the Palace Theatre, Lucy's on Market Street for the town's best independent cafe breakfast, Britalia a few doors down Leeming Street for family Italian cooked from scratch, and the Brown Cow on Ratcliffe Gate for six to ten real ales in a CAMRA Good Beer Guide pub with a real fire. Four moods, one compact market town.

Rather than list everywhere in Mansfield, this guide names four places worth going out of your way for, one in each everyday category, and lets them point you towards the rest of the town.

The Four Picks

Casual

Andwhynot

62 Leeming Street, town centre (next to the Palace Theatre)

Andwhynot has been the anchor independent on Leeming Street for well over a decade, and a £200,000 refurbishment gave it a purpose-built wood-fired pizza kitchen alongside the existing bar and restaurant floors. The result is a broad menu that runs from deli boards and tapas-style starters through stone-baked pizzas from the named oven, Beryl, to steaks, burgers, gourmet pastas and tacos. Sunday roasts draw a loyal crowd, with the pork-and-crackling edition frequently singled out in reviews as among the best in town. Upstairs, the Canvas events room hosts live acoustic evenings, comedy dinners and tribute nights, making it a full evening's venue rather than just a restaurant.

Cafe

Lucy's

16-17 Market Street, town centre (beside the market place)

Lucy's sits at the top of Market Street, a few steps from the open-air market that has traded in Mansfield since Henry III granted the Royal Charter in 1227. It is the kind of independent cafe the town centre does well: a short-hop from the market stalls, serving breakfasts, lunches and daily specials from a rotating board. The specials lean adventurous for a high-street cafe, stretching to Moroccan-spiced lamb burgers and Thai cod and prawn fishcakes alongside the classics. Everything is made in-house, the menu carries gluten-free options, and a food hygiene rating of five confirms the kitchen's standards. Locals have named it the best independent cafe in Mansfield on more than one occasion.

Known for
Daily specials board and a strong local reputation for breakfasts

Restaurant

Britalia

34 Leeming Street, town centre

Britalia is a small family-run Italian that opened on Leeming Street around 2018 and has built a loyal following on the strength of its cooking. The name blends British and Italian, and the menu does the same: dishes are chosen from across Italy and cooked from scratch by chef Romany, who comes from a fine-dining background. Homemade pizza bases, hand-rolled pasta and a standout lobster ravioli are the things reviewers return for, and the room is deliberately small, creating an atmosphere closer to a neighbourhood trattoria than a high-street Italian. The restaurant opens Tuesday to Saturday from 5.30 pm; booking ahead is advised as tables go quickly at weekends. Rated 4.8 on TripAdvisor and updated on Yelp as recently as May 2026.

Pub

The Brown Cow

31 Ratcliffe Gate, town centre

The Brown Cow was resurrected in November 2013 when Everards, the Leicester family brewer, partnered with RAW Brewing of Chesterfield to turn a failing pub into one of the region's serious craft-beer venues. The reopening brought 21 taps covering ales, lagers and ciders, and the pub has been listed in the CAMRA Good Beer Guide every year since, appearing again in the 2026 edition. In early 2025 the pub passed to new owners Jonathan and Matthew Taylor and partner Damann Nichols; Jonathan had worked behind the bar for ten years before buying in. The range now sits at six to ten real ales plus four craft kegs and traditional ciders, and two bar rooms (one with a real fire), a quiet downstairs room and a function room upstairs make it a pub for lingering. The branch twice-yearly beer festival is the town-centre beer event of the year.

Mansfield's Food Scene

Mansfield is Nottinghamshire's largest market town, and its eating out scene is anchored by a compact cluster of independents on and around Leeming Street. The street runs from the 1752 Moot Hall — the old forest court building for Sherwood — toward the Palace Theatre, and it is where the town's most ambitious restaurant openings have settled. Britalia and Andwhynot sit within a few doors of each other; the Brown Cow is a short walk down Ratcliffe Gate.

The open-air market on the Market Place has traded for more than 700 years, since Henry III granted the Royal Charter in 1227. It still runs Tuesday to Saturday and is one of the largest outdoor markets in Nottinghamshire, with fresh produce stalls alongside hot food traders. Lucy's on Market Street sits right beside it and draws much of its trade from market-day visitors. The town's wider food geography tilts toward comfort cooking — roasts, pizza, curry and classic pub food — with the Italian and Mediterranean independents punching above their weight.

Mansfield does not have a famous signature dish in the way that Nottingham has its mushy peas or the Balti Triangle belongs to Birmingham, but it does have a well-defined pub culture. The town sits in CAMRA's Mansfield and Ashfield branch territory, which consistently produces multiple Good Beer Guide entries. For visitors arriving on the train, the town centre is a five-minute walk, and all four venues in this guide are within a fifteen-minute stroll of each other.

Behind the Pass: The Kitchens That Keep Mansfield Fed

Every kitchen in this guide, however small, runs an extraction system - canopy, baffle filters, plenum, ductwork and fan - that pulls heat, smoke and grease-laden air off the cookline. Mansfield is a working market town with a long-established dining strip on Leeming Street; the wood-fired ovens at its pizza bars and the busy dinner services at its Italian restaurants push a steady load of grease-laden vapour into kitchen canopies every evening.

Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the "responsible person" for a premises must assess and manage fire risk, and a grease-laden duct is one of the most serious risks in any catering building. The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) reinforce the same duty of care, while an Environmental Health Officer (EHO) or a buildings insurer can ask to see documented proof the extraction system has been professionally cleaned.

What is TR19 Grease?

TR19 Grease is the UK industry standard for cleaning ductwork and extraction systems in commercial kitchens, published by the Building Engineering Services Association (BESA). It defines how grease deposits are measured, the maximum thickness allowed before cleaning is due, and the post-clean certification a kitchen should hold - the benchmark insurers, fire assessors and EHOs look for.

A small kitchen is no less of a fire risk than a big one. A clean, certified extraction system is what keeps it safe and insured.

Industry Insight

The Cleaning Frequency Most Operators Get Wrong

Here is something many venue owners do not realise: under TR19 Grease, cleaning frequency is set by how many hours the kitchen actually cooks, not by how the canopy looks. A heavy-use kitchen running 12 to 16 hours a day should be cleaned roughly every three months. Moderate use of 6 to 12 hours points to every six months, and light use of 2 to 6 hours to every twelve.

The catch is that baffle filters can look clean while the plenum, the horizontal duct runs and the fan housing carry a heavy grease load that throttles airflow and lowers extract efficiency. A fire in that concealed ductwork spreads fast. That is why a dated TR19 certificate, kept in the fire logbook, is the document an insurer expects to see - and its absence at the point of a claim is a common reason cover is refused.

Planning Your Visit

All four venues cluster in the town centre within easy walking distance of Mansfield railway station (on the Robin Hood Line from Nottingham). Leeming Street, where Andwhynot and Britalia both sit, is a ten-minute walk from the station. Lucy's is on Market Street, one block from the Market Place, and the Brown Cow is on Ratcliffe Gate, a couple of minutes from Leeming Street. There is no need for a car once you are in the centre.

A good day's order: market and coffee at Lucy's in the morning, Andwhynot for lunch or pizza in the afternoon, a circuit of the Brown Cow's real ales in the evening, and Britalia saved for when you want a proper sit-down Italian dinner. Britalia takes bookings and gets busy at weekends, so call ahead. The Brown Cow is cash-free and tends to fill on live-music Saturdays.

  • Start at the open-air market on the Market Place - it runs Tues to Sat
  • Coffee and a specials-board lunch at Lucy's on Market Street
  • Wood-fired pizza from Beryl the oven at Andwhynot, next to the Palace Theatre
  • Book Britalia ahead for a Friday or Saturday Italian dinner on Leeming Street
  • End at the Brown Cow on Ratcliffe Gate for real ales and a real fire

What to Order and How to Do the Day

Order with intent. At Andwhynot, a deli board to share followed by one of Beryl's stone-baked pizzas, or the pork-and-crackling Sunday roast if you are there on a Sunday. At Lucy's, ask what is on the specials board that day - the kitchen earns its reputation there. At Britalia, the lobster ravioli is the dish reviewers single out most often; let the pasta choose itself after that. At the Brown Cow, ask the bar what RAW Brewing is pouring that week and work through the changing ales from there.

Frequently Asked Questions

Mansfield Food and Drink FAQ

What is the best restaurant in Mansfield town centre?

Britalia on Leeming Street is the most consistently praised independent restaurant in the town. The small family Italian opens Tuesday to Saturday from 5.30 pm; chef Romany cooks from scratch and the lobster ravioli and homemade pizza bases are the dishes to order. Book ahead at weekends.

Where can I get a good coffee and lunch in Mansfield?

Lucy's Cafe at 16-17 Market Street is the town centre's best-regarded independent cafe. It sits beside the open-air market and runs a daily specials board alongside breakfasts and lunches, with gluten-free options and a five-star food hygiene rating.

Does Mansfield have a good real ale pub?

The Brown Cow on Ratcliffe Gate has been in the CAMRA Good Beer Guide continuously since it reopened in 2013. It serves six to ten real ales plus craft kegs and ciders, and the Mansfield and Ashfield CAMRA branch holds its twice-yearly beer festival here.

Where is Andwhynot in Mansfield and what does it serve?

Andwhynot is at 62 Leeming Street, next door to the Palace Theatre. It serves wood-fired pizzas from a named oven (Beryl), alongside Mediterranean-influenced plates, steaks, burgers and pastas. It also runs live music and comedy events in its upstairs Canvas room.

How do I get to Mansfield town centre by public transport?

Mansfield railway station is on the Robin Hood Line from Nottingham, with regular services. The town centre is about a ten-minute walk from the station, and all four venues in this guide are within a fifteen-minute stroll of each other.

What is Mansfield market and when does it run?

Mansfield has held a market since Henry III granted the Royal Charter in 1227, and it is one of the largest open-air markets in Nottinghamshire. It runs Tuesday to Saturday in the town-centre Market Place, with fresh produce, hot food traders and general goods stalls.
