Where to Eat in Hemel Hempstead

Quick Answer

For a fast answer: Chiang Mai Cottage on the Old Town High Street for Thai green curry and khao soi in a centuries-old terrace, Hive Coffee Shop for organic Fairtrade coffee in a 500-year-old building, The Cochin for Hertfordshire's first authentic South Indian with Kerala-style dosas, and The Olde Kings Arms for cask ale and stonebaked pizza in a 16th-century inn where Henry VIII is said to have stayed. Four moods, one cobbled High Street.

Hemel Hempstead does not have one food scene so much as several, layered street by street. This guide does something simple: it picks one genuine standout in each of four everyday categories and uses them as doorways into the wider town.

The Four Picks

Casual Dining

Chiang Mai Cottage

80 High Street, Old Town

At the quieter end of the Old Town High Street, Chiang Mai Cottage occupies a row of low Tudor-style terraced buildings that suit its unpretentious cooking perfectly. The restaurant has been a fixture of the street for well over a decade, drawing a loyal local following for Northern Thai home cooking that leans on balance and aromatics rather than novelty. The kitchen's khao soi -- a creamy coconut curry broth with egg noodles and crisp fried-noodle topping -- is a standout, and the green curry maintains a steady herbal heat that does not overpower. A swift takeaway trade runs alongside the sit-in service across three floors. Book ahead on weekends; it fills quickly.

Hive Coffee Shop

17 High Street, Old Town

Hive is tucked into a 500-year-old building on the Old Town High Street and is one of the most characterful coffee stops in Hertfordshire. Owner Kelly runs the place with a clear sustainability brief: the house blend is organic and Fairtrade, with beans from Colombia, Costa Rica and Sumatra roasted locally in Hemel Hempstead. The food follows the same principle -- breakfasts, seasonal lunches and homemade cakes made on site, served in a room whose decor has been put together with visible care. Dog-friendly throughout. On Friday and Saturday evenings Hive switches to a night cafe format, open until 11pm with a small cocktail and wine list, making it the only place on the street that spans breakfast through to a late drink.

The Cochin

61 High Street, Old Town

The Cochin holds a rare distinction: it is the first authentic South Indian restaurant to open in Hertfordshire, and it has spent more than fifteen years making that case on the Old Town High Street. The kitchen is staffed by chefs from Kerala, and the cooking shows it -- dosas, the rice-and-lentil crepes of the Malabar coast, are prepared at the table in crisp, paper-thin rounds quite different from the thick curry-house pancakes found elsewhere. Keralan fish curries, coconut-based vegetable dishes and a long vegetarian menu complete a menu that leans heavily on fish and plant rather than meat. Ranked number one of all Hemel Hempstead restaurants on TripAdvisor and rated 9.7 on TheFork. Book ahead for evenings.

The Olde Kings Arms

High Street, Old Town

The Olde Kings Arms dates to the early 1500s and a plaque records that Henry VIII stayed here during his tenure of the town. By the 1600s it was a well-established alehouse with stabling for 36 horses; today it is a nine-room inn on the prettiest stretch of the Old Town High Street. The kitchen is led by chef Penny, who makes all food on the premises: stonebaked pizzas in partnership with Barrel and Stone, weekend Sunday roasts, and a pub menu that leans into locally sourced produce. Cask ales from Brewpoint and rotating guests are on the bar. The rear beer garden comes into its own in summer and the Sunday roast trade is consistently praised across recent reviews.

Hemel Hempstead's Food Scene

Hemel Hempstead is a town of two halves. The Marlowes is the 1950s new-town centre that the post-war planners laid out across the valley, with chains and shopping. The Old Town is everything that predates it: a cobbled High Street climbing the hillside, lined with Tudor, Georgian and Victorian buildings, and holding almost every independent restaurant and cafe worth knowing in Hemel. Henry VIII granted the town its market charter in 1539 and the High Street has been a trading and eating street ever since.

The food on that street is more interesting than the town's reputation suggests. The Cochin brought authentic Keralan cooking to Hertfordshire at a time when South Indian cuisine was largely absent from the county, and its dosas and coconut curries remain the most distinctive cooking in the postcode. Chiang Mai Cottage has a similarly long-established loyalty: a Thai kitchen rooted in Northern Thai recipes, in a building that was old when the restaurant opened. Hive Coffee Shop completes the picture with an organic roast and a building that predates them both.

Beyond the High Street, the Grand Union Canal runs through nearby Apsley and Boxmoor, with canal-side pubs and the Fishery Inn providing a second cluster of independent hospitality beside the water. The monthly Old Town Sunday Market brings producers and food stalls to the street, and the Hemel Old Town Beer Festival at St Mary's Church -- usually in July -- showcases regional real ale and local street food in the churchyard at the top of the High Street.

Behind the Pass: The Kitchens That Keep Hemel Hempstead Fed

Every kitchen in this guide, however small, runs an extraction system - canopy, baffle filters, plenum, ductwork and fan - that pulls heat, smoke and grease-laden air off the cookline. Hemel Hempstead's Old Town kitchens run the full range from a Thai wok searing at high heat to a pub kitchen turning out roasts and stonebaked pizzas every service, and every one of those high-temperature cooking lines loads grease-laden vapour into canopies that must be kept clear.

Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the "responsible person" for a premises must assess and manage fire risk, and a grease-laden duct is one of the most serious risks in any catering building. The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) reinforce the same duty of care, while an Environmental Health Officer (EHO) or a buildings insurer can ask to see documented proof the extraction system has been professionally cleaned.

What is TR19 Grease?

TR19 Grease is the UK industry standard for cleaning ductwork and extraction systems in commercial kitchens, published by the Building Engineering Services Association (BESA). It defines how grease deposits are measured, the maximum thickness allowed before cleaning is due, and the post-clean certification a kitchen should hold - the benchmark insurers, fire assessors and EHOs look for.

A small kitchen is no less of a fire risk than a big one. A clean, certified extraction system is what keeps it safe and insured.

Industry Insight

The Cleaning Frequency Most Operators Get Wrong

Here is something many venue owners do not realise: under TR19 Grease, cleaning frequency is set by how many hours the kitchen actually cooks, not by how the canopy looks. A heavy-use kitchen running 12 to 16 hours a day should be cleaned roughly every three months. Moderate use of 6 to 12 hours points to every six months, and light use of 2 to 6 hours to every twelve.

The catch is that baffle filters can look clean while the plenum, the horizontal duct runs and the fan housing carry a heavy grease load that throttles airflow and lowers extract efficiency. A fire in that concealed ductwork spreads fast. That is why a dated TR19 certificate, kept in the fire logbook, is the document an insurer expects to see - and its absence at the point of a claim is a common reason cover is refused.

Planning Your Visit

The Old Town is compact and entirely walkable. The High Street runs uphill from the bottom of the old town to St Mary's Church at the top, no more than ten minutes end to end, with Chiang Mai Cottage, The Cochin, Hive and The Olde Kings Arms all within a few doors of each other. Hemel Hempstead station is about fifteen minutes on foot from the Old Town, or a five-minute taxi ride.

The natural order is coffee at Hive, lunch at one of the restaurants, and an evening divided between The Cochin for the food and The Olde Kings Arms for the pint. If you time it right the Friday or Saturday night cafe at Hive extends the evening further. For the Beer Festival or the Sunday Market, check the Old Town BID website for dates -- both draw visitors from across Dacorum and beyond.

  • Walk the Old Town High Street from bottom to top to get the measure of it
  • Book The Cochin in advance for evenings -- it fills, especially at weekends
  • Try the khao soi at Chiang Mai Cottage: it is the most distinctive dish on the street
  • Visit Hive on a Friday or Saturday evening when it runs as a night cafe until 11pm
  • Check the Olde Kings Arms for the Sunday roast -- chef Penny's food is praised in recent reviews

What to Order and How to Do the Day

Order with intent. At Chiang Mai Cottage, the khao soi: the coconut-curry broth with egg noodles and crisp toppings is the kitchen at its best. At Hive, ask which single-origin is in the blend that week and try the homemade cake. At The Cochin, start with a dosa -- the paper-thin Keralan version -- then follow with a fish curry from the coastal menu. At The Olde Kings Arms, a stonebaked pizza from the Barrel and Stone menu and a pint of Brewpoint cask ale, taken in the beer garden in summer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Hemel Hempstead Food and Drink FAQ

Where is the best place to eat in Hemel Hempstead Old Town?

The Old Town High Street holds almost all of Hemel Hempstead's best independents within a short walk: The Cochin for South Indian, Chiang Mai Cottage for Thai, Hive Coffee Shop for speciality coffee, and The Olde Kings Arms for cask ale and pub food in a 16th-century inn.

Does Hemel Hempstead have good independent restaurants?

Yes, concentrated in the Old Town. The Cochin is ranked number one on TripAdvisor for Hemel Hempstead and is the first authentic South Indian restaurant in Hertfordshire. Chiang Mai Cottage has served Thai home cooking for over a decade in a Tudor terrace.

What is the best South Indian restaurant in Hertfordshire?

The Cochin at 61 High Street in Hemel Hempstead Old Town is widely recognised as the first authentic South Indian restaurant in the county. The kitchen is staffed by chefs from Kerala and the dosas and fish curries are the standout dishes.

Which pub in Hemel Hempstead has the best history?

The Olde Kings Arms on the Old Town High Street dates to the early 1500s and a plaque records Henry VIII as a guest. It serves cask ales from Brewpoint, stonebaked pizzas and Sunday roasts, and has nine B and B rooms above the bar.

Is there a good coffee shop in Hemel Hempstead Old Town?

Hive Coffee Shop at 17 High Street is an organic and Fairtrade coffee shop set in a 500-year-old building. The house blend is roasted locally in Hemel Hempstead; it also opens as a night cafe on Friday and Saturday evenings.

What is the Old Town Sunday Market in Hemel Hempstead?

The Hemel Old Town Sunday Market is a monthly market on the cobbled High Street featuring local food producers, artisan goods and street food stalls. The annual Old Town Beer Festival at St Mary's Church, usually in July, adds over 30 real ales from regional brewers to the calendar.
