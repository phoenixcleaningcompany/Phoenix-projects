Ten things that commonly get checked after a commercial kitchen fire claim:

1. A valid, in-date TR19 grease extraction certificate
2. A fire risk assessment that's actually been reviewed recently, not just filed away
3. Records showing when the extraction system was last properly cleaned, and by whom
4. Fire doors in working order, not wedged open

7. Any fitted fire suppression system serviced within its required interval
8. Portable appliance (PAT) testing up to date

## Email 2 — "A wipe-down isn't a TR19 clean" (10-14 days later, no reply)

Subject: Why a wipe-down isn't a TR19 clean — *|SITE|*

Hi there,

Following up from my last note — thought I'd explain a distinction that catches a lot of kitchens out.

Kitchen staff cleaning the canopy and visible surfaces daily is genuinely good practice, but it only reaches what you can see and wipe by hand. Grease builds up inside the ductwork, the plenum (a hidden trap most people don't know exists), and the extractor fan itself — none of which a normal clean touches. A proper TR19 clean strips the canopy, cleans the baffle filters, clears the plenum, and reaches the full duct run via access hatches, with before/after photos at each stage.

The two aren't a substitute for each other. Daily cleaning keeps the kitchen presentable; a TR19 clean is what actually reduces the fire risk in the system itself and gives you a certificate an insurer or EHO will accept.

If it's been a while since your system had a proper TR19 clean rather than a surface wipe-down, happy to take a look — no obligation.
