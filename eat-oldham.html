Where to Eat in Oldham

Quick Answer

For a fast answer: Waterhead Tandoori on Huddersfield Road for freshly prepared South Asian tandoori and biryani, Kobe Coffee on Uppermill High Street for speciality espresso in a Saddleworth village cafe opened by England rugby brothers, Lees Spice Lounge in Lees for the 2025 National Curry Awards North West winner, and The White Hart at Lydgate for a Michelin-listed gastropub inside a Grade II-listed inn built in 1788. Four moods, one borough.

There is far more to eat in Oldham than any one page could hold, so this guide keeps it honest: one real, independent standout per category - somewhere to grab a quick bite, a proper coffee, a meal out and a pint - chosen on merit.

The Four Picks

Takeaway/Casual

Waterhead Tandoori

491 Huddersfield Road, Waterhead

Waterhead Tandoori has been feeding the eastern edge of Oldham from a terrace house frontage on Huddersfield Road for years, building a loyal following in the Waterhead neighbourhood close to Dovestone Reservoir. It runs a fully halal kitchen, marinating chicken tikka and seekh kebabs in the tandoor before serving them alongside a wide menu of freshly prepared curries and biryanis. Regulars speak most warmly about the kebabs — the mixed shish and chicken tikka are considered among the best in the borough. A nomination for the English Curry Awards' Best Takeaway and Delivery category in 2024 reflects the esteem it commands locally. It opens every evening from 4:30 pm and offers delivery across Oldham. The premises licence was renewed through 2026.

Kobe Coffee

36 High Street, Uppermill, Saddleworth

Kobe Coffee opened in early 2020 when brothers Joe and George Ford transformed a former fish and chip restaurant on Uppermill's High Street with a £97,000 HSBC package. The brothers grew up in Grasscroft, both played rugby league for Saddleworth Rangers before turning professional — George Ford went on to represent England — and named the cafe after Joe's son. The coffee is sourced from Outpost Coffee Roasters, who work directly with indigenous communities protecting South American rainforest. Outpost trained baristas pull espresso, filter and flat whites from 9 am; brunch and lunch run all day, seven days a week, with Eggs Benedict and full cooked breakfasts on the menu alongside avocado toast and seasonal plates. A sister restaurant, Kobe Pan Asia, operates from the same building in the evenings from Wednesday.

Lees Spice Lounge

73 High Street, Lees, Oldham

Mr Rahman opened Lees Spice Lounge around 2008, building on the success of Lees Tandoori takeaway — a village institution he had been running for over three decades on the same stretch of High Street. The Spice Lounge sits in the heart of the village of Lees, which lies between the Oldham town centre and the Saddleworth hills, and draws diners from across the borough for its pan-subcontinent menu: dishes from Bangladesh, India, Nepal, Sri Lanka and the Punjab are all represented. The Naga Jal-Fry with keema naan is the dish regulars come back for. The restaurant was named Curry House of the Year for the North West at the National Curry Awards 2025, held on 20 October 2025, cementing its standing as the best curry dining room in the region. It opens evenings only, Tuesday to Sunday, and takes bookings.

The White Hart at Lydgate

51 Stockport Road, Lydgate, Saddleworth

The White Hart was built in 1788 by local landowner John Buckley as a weavers' cottage in the Saddleworth hilltop village of Lydgate, later serving — at different points in its long life — as a police station with a jail, a schoolhouse, and a wartime lookout. It is now Grade II listed and one of the most awarded gastropubs in the North West. Head chef Nathan Sidebottom, who grew up in Oldham and trained at the White Hart himself from the age of eighteen before honing his skills at two-Michelin-starred Midsummer House, returned in 2020 and has since won two AA Rosettes for the kitchen. The pub appears in the 2026 Michelin Guide and was ranked in the Top 50 Gastropubs Awards 2026. The dining rooms — the Brasserie, the Old Tap Room and a Barn Room — run from classic pub food through to pan-fried scallops with lentil dhal and black cod with truffle. There are sixteen en-suite bedrooms for those who want to stay.

Oldham's Food Scene

Oldham's food map splits along a clear geographic line. The town centre and the inner districts of Glodwick and Werneth — where South Asian communities settled from the 1960s onward to work in the mills — hold a dense collection of curry houses, Pakistani grills and halal takeaways. Tommyfield Market, in the town centre since the eighteenth century (and rehousing in the Spindles shopping centre in 2026), has always been the heart of the town's food economy, a place for pies, oven-bottom muffins and local produce. Oldham even lays claim to the world's first fish and chip shop, said to have opened here around 1858.

Head east into the Saddleworth villages — Uppermill, Lees, Lydgate, Delph, Dobcross — and the food changes register entirely. Saddleworth is a string of former mill villages strung along the Tame valley at the foot of the Pennines, each with a pub or cafe that serves the hikers, cyclists and daytrippers who come from Manchester for the moorland air. The Saddleworth Farmers Market in Uppermill runs through the year, offering local cheese, meat, bread and produce.

The borough's most distinctive local dish is the Oldham rag pudding: seasoned minced meat wrapped in suet pastry — not a bowl or a cloth now, but a very specific Oldham tradition still served in a handful of market stalls and cafes. At the other end of the spectrum, the string of curry restaurants in Lees village, anchored by Lees Spice Lounge, has built a reputation that draws diners from across Greater Manchester, culminating in a National Curry Award in 2025.

Behind the Pass: The Kitchens That Keep Oldham Fed

Behind every bar in this guide is a kitchen, and behind every kitchen an extraction system - canopy, baffle filters, plenum, ductwork and fan - drawing grease-laden air off the cookline and out through the roof. Oldham's kitchens range from late-night South Asian tandoors in Glodwick and Waterhead to the wood-fired grills and brasserie ranges of the Saddleworth gastropubs — every service pushes a heavy load of grease-laden vapour through canopies that need to meet TR19 standards to keep insurer and EHO satisfied.

Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the "responsible person" for a premises must assess and manage fire risk, and a grease-laden duct is one of the most serious risks in any catering building. The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) reinforce the same duty of care, while an Environmental Health Officer (EHO) or a buildings insurer can ask to see documented proof the extraction system has been professionally cleaned.

What is TR19 Grease?

TR19 Grease is the UK industry standard for cleaning ductwork and extraction systems in commercial kitchens, published by the Building Engineering Services Association (BESA). It defines how grease deposits are measured, the maximum thickness allowed before cleaning is due, and the post-clean certification a kitchen should hold - the benchmark insurers, fire assessors and EHOs look for.

A great pub kitchen lives or dies on its food. The extraction system is what keeps it open, insured and safe behind the scenes.

Industry Insight

The Cleaning Frequency Most Operators Get Wrong

Here is something many venue owners do not realise: under TR19 Grease, cleaning frequency is set by how many hours the kitchen actually cooks, not by how the canopy looks. A heavy-use kitchen running 12 to 16 hours a day should be cleaned roughly every three months. Moderate use of 6 to 12 hours points to every six months, and light use of 2 to 6 hours to every twelve.

The catch is that baffle filters can look clean while the plenum, the horizontal duct runs and the fan housing carry a heavy grease load that throttles airflow and lowers extract efficiency. A fire in that concealed ductwork spreads fast. That is why a dated TR19 certificate, kept in the fire logbook, is the document an insurer expects to see - and its absence at the point of a claim is a common reason cover is refused.

Planning Your Visit

The geography means you will need a car or bus for the full tour. Waterhead is about a mile and a half east of the town centre along Huddersfield Road; Lees is a further mile beyond that, and the Saddleworth villages — Uppermill and Lydgate — sit another two to three miles into the hills. The 180 and 184 buses run from Oldham Mumps to Uppermill via Lees and Waterhead. Driving through the valley, the hills open up gradually as you pass through each village.

A natural day works east from the town: coffee and brunch at Kobe Coffee in Uppermill in the morning, a walk along the Huddersfield Narrow Canal or up to Dovestone Reservoir, then the short drive to Lees for a curry dinner at Lees Spice Lounge. Save The White Hart at Lydgate for a weekend lunch, when the brasserie is at its best and the views across the valley reward a slow afternoon. Kobe Coffee is open daily from 9 am; Lees Spice Lounge and the White Hart both benefit from advance booking.

  • Arrive at Kobe Coffee in Uppermill from 9 am - the brunch menu runs all day
  • Book Lees Spice Lounge ahead for evenings; it fills fast since the 2025 curry award
  • Reserve a table at The White Hart for weekend lunch to catch the valley views
  • Try the Naga Jal-Fry with keema naan at Lees Spice Lounge - the house signature
  • Take the 180 or 184 bus from Oldham Mumps to reach Uppermill and Lees without a car

What to Order and How to Do the Day

Order with intent. At Waterhead Tandoori, the mixed shish kebab and chicken tikka straight from the tandoor, with a karahi or biryani alongside. At Kobe Coffee, a flat white on Outpost-roasted espresso and Eggs Benedict while they last in the morning rush. At Lees Spice Lounge, start with the Naga Jal-Fry — the signature that won the 2025 North West curry award — paired with keema naan; explore the Sri Lankan and Nepali sections of the menu for something less common. At The White Hart, a pint of local cask ale in the Old Tap Room first, then the scallops with lentil dhal or the black cod with truffle in the brasserie, letting Nathan Sidebottom's two-rosette kitchen do the work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Oldham Food and Drink FAQ

What is Oldham famous for in terms of food?

Oldham has two food claims: it is said to be the birthplace of the fish and chip shop, with the first reportedly opening near Tommyfield Market around 1858, and it is home to the Oldham rag pudding, a suet-wrapped seasoned mince pudding still served at local market stalls. The borough also has a deep South Asian food tradition rooted in Glodwick, Werneth and the Lees village curry houses.

Which Oldham restaurant won a national curry award?

Lees Spice Lounge at 73 High Street in Lees won Curry House of the Year for the North West at the National Curry Awards 2025, held on 20 October 2025. The restaurant has been run by Mr Rahman since around 2008 and serves a pan-subcontinent menu spanning Bangladesh, India, Nepal, Sri Lanka and the Punjab. Booking ahead for evening sittings is recommended.

Is The White Hart at Lydgate in the Michelin Guide?

Yes. The White Hart at 51 Stockport Road, Lydgate, appears in the 2026 Michelin Guide United Kingdom and was ranked in the Top 50 Gastropubs Awards 2026. It also holds two AA Rosettes, earned under head chef Nathan Sidebottom, who joined in 2020 after working at two-Michelin-starred Midsummer House. The Grade II-listed building dates to 1788.

Who opened Kobe Coffee in Uppermill?

Kobe Coffee at 36 High Street, Uppermill, was opened in early 2020 by brothers Joe and George Ford, both from Grasscroft in Saddleworth. George Ford is an England rugby union international who played for Leicester Tigers; Joe also played professional rugby. They named the cafe after Joe's son Kobe. The coffee is sourced from Outpost Coffee Roasters, who work with indigenous South American communities. Kobe Coffee is open daily from 9 am.

Where are the best South Asian restaurants in Oldham?

The South Asian food heartland runs from the inner districts of Glodwick and Werneth — where Pakistani and Bangladeshi communities settled from the 1960s onward — through to the village of Lees, where Lees Spice Lounge (National Curry Awards 2025 North West winner) sits alongside the longer-established Lees Tandoori takeaway. Waterhead Tandoori on Huddersfield Road in Waterhead is also consistently rated for its kebabs and biryani.

How do I get to Saddleworth from Oldham town centre?

The 180 and 184 buses run from Oldham Mumps interchange east along Huddersfield Road, stopping at Waterhead and Lees before continuing to Uppermill in about 25 minutes. By car the A669 takes you from the town centre through Lees and into Saddleworth in under fifteen minutes. Lydgate, home to The White Hart, is a short drive south of Uppermill on the Stockport Road.
