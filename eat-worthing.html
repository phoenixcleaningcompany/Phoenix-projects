Where to Eat in Worthing

Quick Answer

For a fast answer: Chipwick on Brighton Road for the three-generation family chippy that has won Best Fish and Chips nine times, CrabShack on Marine Parade for locally landed seafood metres from the sea, Ami on Rowlands Road for the Come Dine with Me-winning modern British bistro, and The Corner House on the High Street for a free house built on Sussex brewery ales and a Sunday roast. Four moods, one seafront town.

Rather than list everywhere in Worthing, this guide names four places worth going out of your way for, one in each everyday category, and lets them point you towards the rest of the town.

The Four Picks

Takeaway/Casual

Chipwick

94 Brighton Road, south Worthing

Chipwick has been frying on Brighton Road since 1981, when Jeffrey and Sandra Rance opened the shop and built a reputation for light, consistent batter and the prime cut of cod — the thicker loin behind the head, which stays moist under heat. Their son Adam and his wife Becky took over in 2003, and their children Beth and Ollie have since joined, making this a third-generation family business. The awards have followed every decade: nine Best Fish and Chips wins in all, including a Guardian Shop of the Year for the local area in 1993 and the 2025-26 Good Food Award Gold Seal, given only to businesses with three consecutive Good Food Award wins. The fully licensed restaurant seats 40, and the bespoke Florigo range ensures every piece is fried to the same standard.

Known for
A 40-year family recipe, nine best-chippy awards including 2025-26 Good Food Gold Seal
Good for

Cafe

Finch Bar & Eatery

27 Warwick Street, town centre

Warwick Street is Worthing's pedestrianised food strip, a minute from the seafront, and Finch anchors the daytime end of it. The all-day brunch menu runs from 8am, with luxury breakfast plates alongside shareable small dishes, locally roasted coffee — the flat whites are the order — and a drinks list that slides from craft beer and Prosecco into cocktails as the afternoon wears on. Ranked consistently in the top ten restaurants in Worthing and holder of a TripAdvisor Travellers Choice award in 2025, Finch has earned its position as the default mid-morning stop on Warwick Street. Private dining is available for small groups, and the room has a relaxed, buzzing feel that keeps it full on weekend mornings.

Known for
Luxury all-day brunch, locally roasted flat whites, Warwick Street atmosphere
Good for
A long weekend brunch or a mid-morning coffee before hitting the seafront
Restaurant

Ami

93b Rowlands Road, residential Worthing

Ami opened in June 2014 on a residential side street a short walk from the promenade, and chef Amy Cowen has built it into the town's most praised independent restaurant over more than a decade. The seasonally changing menu uses local and Sussex produce across lunch and dinner, refreshed quarterly so regulars always find something new. In 2023 the restaurant appeared on Channel 4's Come Dine with Me: The Professionals and took first prize, with a three-course menu of smoked haddock and mackerel, roasted rump of lamb and chocolate torte that judges called first-class. Ami reopened in January 2026 after a full refurbishment with a new a la carte menu. It is open Wednesday to Saturday, lunch and dinner, and tables book up quickly.

Known for
Come Dine with Me-winning modern British cooking, quarterly seasonal menu
Good for
A proper occasion dinner in a neighbourhood room with real cooking ambition
Pub

The Corner House

80 High Street, town centre

A pub has stood on this corner of the High Street since at least 1805, when guides recorded a venue called The Anchor. The current building dates from around 1895 and became The Corner House in 2015, run as a free house with a principled focus on Sussex breweries and independent wine suppliers. Regulars find rotating cask ales from Goldmark, Harvey's Sussex Best Bitter, and other local brewers on the bar, all kept by a team that takes cellaring seriously. Head chef Dean's kitchen leans into British seasonal cooking: the Sunday roast with locally sourced meat is the weekly draw, with chicken-wing nights on Wednesdays and a Thursday Pie of the Day filling out the week. A heated garden with pods extends the season outdoors, and a log-burning stove keeps the bar cosy through winter.

Worthing's Food Scene

Worthing spent most of the twentieth century as a quiet retirement seaside town, but its food scene has shifted sharply in the last decade. The seafront strip — Marine Parade eastward from the pier — now holds some of the best-regarded independent restaurants on the Sussex coast, with CrabShack the flagship: a three-time winner of the BRAVO Best Sussex Restaurant award, ranked among Europe's top ten beach restaurants by The Guardian, and buzzing on summer evenings with tables overlooking the shingle.

Away from the seafront, Warwick Street is the town's pedestrianised dining strip, lined with independent cafes, brunch spots and bars. Montague Street runs parallel, with more variety — Japanese street food, pizza and Indian — while the residential streets off the centre hide neighbourhood gems like Ami on Rowlands Road, the kind of place locals guard as a local secret. The historic village of West Tarring, a mile north of the seafront, retains its own food character, with the 13th-century Parsonage Row and a pair of traditional pubs.

Fish and chips remain the town's signature eat, and Worthing is serious about them: Chipwick on Brighton Road has been winning national chippy awards since 1993. The wider scene is becoming increasingly ambitious — Ami's Come Dine with Me win in 2023 put the town's fine-dining credentials on the national map, and new venues have opened steadily along the seafront and in the centre. The pier itself is home to the new Perch restaurant, adding another elevated dining option with sea views.

Behind the Pass: The Kitchens That Keep Worthing Fed

Fryers, grills and pass after pass through a busy season put a commercial kitchen's extraction system - canopy, baffle filters, plenum, ductwork and fan - under real strain, drawing grease-laden vapour out of the building. Worthing's seafront restaurants and busy seasonal fryers push a heavy load of grease-laden vapour through their canopies from spring bank holiday to the last warm weekend of autumn, and a seafront chippy running lunchtime and evening service back-to-back carries one of the highest grease loads on the south coast.

Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the "responsible person" for a premises must assess and manage fire risk, and a grease-laden duct is one of the most serious risks in any catering building. The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) reinforce the same duty of care, while an Environmental Health Officer (EHO) or a buildings insurer can ask to see documented proof the extraction system has been professionally cleaned.

What is TR19 Grease?

TR19 Grease is the UK industry standard for cleaning ductwork and extraction systems in commercial kitchens, published by the Building Engineering Services Association (BESA). It defines how grease deposits are measured, the maximum thickness allowed before cleaning is due, and the post-clean certification a kitchen should hold - the benchmark insurers, fire assessors and EHOs look for.

A seaside kitchen earns its year in a few hot months. A clean extraction system is what keeps it trading through every one of them.

Industry Insight

The Cleaning Frequency Most Operators Get Wrong

Here is something many venue owners do not realise: under TR19 Grease, cleaning frequency is set by how many hours the kitchen actually cooks, not by how the canopy looks. A heavy-use kitchen running 12 to 16 hours a day should be cleaned roughly every three months. Moderate use of 6 to 12 hours points to every six months, and light use of 2 to 6 hours to every twelve.

The catch is that baffle filters can look clean while the plenum, the horizontal duct runs and the fan housing carry a heavy grease load that throttles airflow and lowers extract efficiency. A fire in that concealed ductwork spreads fast. That is why a dated TR19 certificate, kept in the fire logbook, is the document an insurer expects to see - and its absence at the point of a claim is a common reason cover is refused.

Planning Your Visit

Most of this guide is walkable from the seafront. Chipwick is on Brighton Road, a 10-minute walk inland from the pier; Finch is on Warwick Street, one minute from the promenade; and The Corner House is on the High Street in the town centre, five minutes from the beach. Ami on Rowlands Road is a 15-minute walk from the seafront, or a short taxi.

Worthing station is a 10-minute walk from the seafront and has regular direct services from Brighton (25 minutes) and London Victoria (75 minutes). The pier and promenade are the natural orientation point. CrabShack sits right on Marine Parade just east of the pier — go early on summer evenings or book ahead, as the terrace fills quickly. The seafront has free parking behind the beach, but the town centre car parks are more useful for Chipwick and The Corner House.

  • Book CrabShack well in advance for a summer evening seafront table
  • Start at Finch on Warwick Street for brunch and locally roasted coffee
  • Pick up fish and chips from Chipwick on Brighton Road — eat on the beach
  • Reserve Ami on Rowlands Road for the occasion dinner; it books out weeks ahead
  • End at The Corner House for a Harvey's or Goldmark ale from a proper Sussex cellar

What to Order and How to Do the Day

Order with intent. At Chipwick, a regular cod in the famous light batter with proper chips — ask for the prime loin cut. At Finch, a luxury brunch plate and a flat white with locally roasted beans. At Ami, trust the seasonal a la carte: the kitchen refreshes it quarterly so whatever is on is what is best right now. At The Corner House, whatever Harvey's or Goldmark ale is on the cask that week, alongside a Sunday roast if you time it right.

Frequently Asked Questions

Worthing Food and Drink FAQ

Where is the best fish and chips in Worthing?

Chipwick at 94 Brighton Road is the town's most decorated chippy, with nine Best Fish and Chips awards and the 2025-26 Good Food Award Gold Seal. The three-generation family business has been frying since 1981, using the prime loin cut of cod for consistent results. It is fully licensed and seats 40 inside.

What is CrabShack Worthing and why is it so popular?

CrabShack at 2 Marine Parade is a family-run seafood restaurant opened in June 2015 by two families — Sarah and Andy Tinker-Taylor, and Lynda and Phil Racz-Taylor. It sources daily catch from the Sussex coast, has a large sea-facing terrace, and has won the BRAVO Best Sussex Restaurant award three times, including 2025.

Is there a fine-dining restaurant in Worthing?

Yes. Ami at 93b Rowlands Road is Worthing's most awarded independent restaurant, open since 2014 and run by chef Amy Cowen. It won Channel 4's Come Dine with Me: The Professionals and offers a seasonally changing modern British menu, open Wednesday to Saturday for lunch and dinner.

What is the best pub in Worthing for real ale?

The Corner House at 80 High Street is a free house on the site of a pub that has stood since at least 1805. It focuses exclusively on Sussex breweries and independent suppliers, rotating cask ales from Goldmark, Harvey's Sussex Best Bitter and others, with a cellar kept to a high standard.

Where should I eat in Worthing town centre for brunch?

Finch Bar and Eatery at 27 Warwick Street is the town's favourite all-day brunch spot, serving luxury breakfast plates from 8am alongside locally roasted coffee. It holds a TripAdvisor Travellers Choice award and is ranked consistently in the top ten restaurants in Worthing.

Is Worthing worth visiting for food?

Yes. The seafront has CrabShack, a nationally ranked seafood restaurant, alongside the pier-top Perch. Warwick Street covers daytime dining. Ami offers genuine fine dining. Chipwick has been a destination chippy for four decades. The town is compact and walkable, with Worthing station 25 minutes from Brighton and 75 minutes from London.
