How to Maintain a Combi Oven So It Lasts a Decade

Combi oven care

A combi oven is one of the most expensive single items in your kitchen, and it will either repay you for ten years or quietly fail at eight - the difference is almost entirely down to how you look after it.

Manufacturers design these machines to run hard, but they assume a level of daily attention that busy kitchens rarely give. The good news is that the routine is short, cheap and easy to teach. Get the water right, keep the seal clean and stay ahead of limescale, and you turn a costly gamble into a decade of dependable service. Here is exactly what to do, and why each step matters.

The maintenance rhythm that protects a combi oven

Reliability comes from repetition, not heroics. Split the work into daily, weekly and periodic tasks, print the lists and pin them by the oven so every shift knows what good looks like.

Every shift & every day

  • Run the oven's automatic clean or wash cycle at the end of service - never leave grease to bake on overnight.
  • Wipe the interior, the door glass and the cavity of loose food and fat while the chamber is still warm.
  • Empty and rinse the drip tray and check the drain is running freely, so standing water and odours never build up.
  • Inspect the door gasket for splits, hardening or grease, and wipe it with a damp cloth - a failing seal is the most common cause of heat loss and uneven cooking.
  • Leave the door ajar overnight so the cavity vents and the gasket dries, which measurably extends the life of the seal.

Every week

  • Deep clean the racks, trays, probe and any removable baffles or fan guards.
  • Clean the internal fan and air path so airflow stays even and the motor is not fighting a coating of grease.
  • Check the water filter or softener for pressure drop or a spent cartridge, and log the date.
  • Wipe exterior panels, controls and the touchscreen, and check the vent above the oven is clear.
  • Confirm the descale or care indicator on the display is not being ignored shift after shift.

Every month & quarter

  • Run a full descale or care cycle with the manufacturer's approved tablets, sized to your water hardness and hours of use.
  • Replace water filter cartridges on schedule - typically every three to six months, sooner in hard-water areas.
  • Have the steam generator descaled every six to twelve months depending on usage.
  • Test door hinges, catches and the seal compression, and note anything that needs a spare part before it fails mid-service.
  • Book planned preventative maintenance at least once a year, or twice a year on a high-volume site.

Why water is the whole game

If you fix only one thing on this page, fix your water. Limescale is the single biggest killer of combi ovens, and it is entirely a function of what comes out of your mains supply. Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium, and every time that water is boiled into steam the minerals are left behind as scale on the heating elements, the steam generator and the internal pipework.

The Drinking Water Inspectorate classifies UK water by its calcium carbonate content, measured in milligrams per litre of CaCO₂. Below 100 mg/l is soft, 100 to 200 mg/l is moderately hard, 200 to 300 mg/l is hard, and anything above 300 mg/l is very hard. The World Health Organisation notes that water at 200 mg/l or above will readily form scale. The UK average sits around 200 ppm, and chalk and limestone regions routinely run at 250 to 400 ppm - firmly in the very hard bracket. You can check your own figure with your water company or by postcode in minutes, and you should, because it dictates everything that follows.

The cost of ignoring it is not abstract. In hard-water areas, unchecked scale can cut a combi oven's heating efficiency by around 12% in as little as four months. That is more gas or electricity for the same tray of food, longer cook times, patchy steam, and elements that burn out early because they are working through an insulating crust. Left long enough, scale blocks solenoids and probes and turns a cleanable machine into a repair bill.

Getting the water right

The fix is a two-part defence. First, install proper filtration or softening on the feed to the oven - most manufacturers specify a target hardness and will not honour warranty claims for scale damage if you run unfiltered mains water. Use a WRAS-approved fitting and size the cartridge to your hardness and throughput, not to whatever was cheapest. Second, keep to the descale routine even with filtration in place, because no filter is perfect and the care cycles clear what slips through. Watch the oven's own care indicator - it counts cycles and warns you before scale becomes a problem, so treating it as a genuine instruction rather than a nuisance light is half the battle.

The seal, the fan and the small parts

After water, the door gasket earns the most attention for the least effort. It is a rubber seal living in a chamber that swings between ambient and well over 250°C, and grease bakes onto it fast. A clean, supple gasket holds steam and heat where they belong; a hardened or torn one leaks, so the oven runs longer and hotter to hit temperature and your energy bill climbs. Wipe it daily, keep the door vented overnight, and replace it the moment it stops sealing - a gasket is a cheap part compared with the cooking faults a bad one causes.

The same logic runs through the whole machine. A grease-choked fan makes the motor labour and cooks unevenly. A blocked drain backs dirty water into the cavity. A sticking door catch strains the hinges. None of these are dramatic on their own, but each one shortens the life of something expensive, and all of them are caught by the weekly and monthly checks above. This is the same discipline that keeps any hard-worked appliance going - the thinking behind buying a commercial fryer that lasts is identical: choose well, then maintain relentlessly.

Servicing, records and the payoff

Daily care handles the routine, but a combi oven still needs a qualified engineer. Book planned preventative maintenance at least annually, and twice a year if you are running back-to-back service on a busy site. A proper service checks the steam generator, tests safety cut-outs, verifies calibration so your recipes actually hit temperature, and replaces worn seals and filters before they strand you. Keep a simple log - filter changes, descale dates, parts replaced, engineer visits - because it proves the machine has been looked after, protects your warranty, and tells you when a fault is a one-off or a pattern.

Here is the number that makes all of this worthwhile. An intensively used combi oven that is neglected often needs replacing after around eight years. The same oven, given planned preventative maintenance, filtered water and daily attention, routinely reaches twelve years or more. That is several extra years of a five-figure asset earning its keep, plus lower energy bills and far fewer mid-service breakdowns along the way. The routine on this page costs a few minutes a shift and a modest service contract; the alternative costs you the oven.

Maintenance also starts before you buy. Specifying a machine that suits your volume, your water and your menu makes every one of these tasks easier, which is why it pays to think carefully about how to choose a combi oven for a high-volume site at the outset rather than inheriting someone else's compromise.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How often should a commercial combi oven be descaled?

It depends on your water hardness and how many hours the oven runs. As a rule, run a manufacturer-approved care or descale cycle monthly, and have the steam generator descaled by an engineer every six to twelve months. In hard-water areas above 200 mg/l calcium carbonate, lean towards the shorter end of every interval, because scale forms faster the harder your water is.

Does water filtration really extend a combi oven's life?

Yes, and it is the single most important thing you can do. Hard water deposits limescale on the elements and steam generator every time it boils, and in hard-water areas that can cut heating efficiency by around 12% within four months. A correctly sized, WRAS-approved filter or softener sized to your hardness keeps scale at bay, protects your warranty, and is far cheaper than the repairs scale causes.

How long should a commercial combi oven last?

An intensively used combi oven that is neglected often needs replacing after around eight years. With planned preventative maintenance, filtered water and daily cleaning, the same machine routinely reaches twelve years or more. The routine costs a few minutes per shift and an annual service, so the extra years of service are effectively free by comparison.

Why does the door gasket matter so much?

The gasket is what holds steam and heat inside the cavity. When it hardens, splits or clogs with grease, the oven leaks and has to run longer and hotter to reach temperature, which pushes up energy use and causes uneven cooking. Wipe it daily, leave the door ajar overnight so it dries, and replace it the moment it stops sealing - it is a cheap part relative to the faults a bad one creates.
