Where to Eat in Birkenhead

Quick Answer

For a fast answer: Oxton Bar and Kitchen in Oxton Village for locally sourced plates and craft ales in an independent bar, Twenty Sixteen Coffee and Kitchen for the Good Food Award cafe in the heart of the village, Daise by Tony Parkin for a Michelin-listed tasting menu at one of the Wirral's most talked-about tables, and Gallaghers Traditional Pub for cask ales in a free house a short walk from the Woodside Ferry. Four moods, one town.

Birkenhead does not have one food scene so much as several, layered street by street. This guide does something simple: it picks one genuine standout in each of four everyday categories and uses them as doorways into the wider town.

The Four Picks

Casual

Oxton Bar and Kitchen

2 Claughton Firs, Oxton Village

Set in the heart of Oxton Village conservation area since 2004, the Oxton Bar and Kitchen is the independent family-run bar that anchors the village's food scene. The kitchen uses locally sourced produce throughout, with a menu that moves between Menai mussels, char-grilled steaks and a Thai green curry without trying too hard, and the bar backs it up with cask ales from Wirral micro-breweries, boutique craft beers and classic cocktails sourced from small independent suppliers. The terrace at the front fills on sunny afternoons; the inside stays warm through the week. No mainstream brands, no compromise on sourcing: this is exactly the kind of independent the village has built its reputation on.

Cafe

Twenty Sixteen Coffee and Kitchen

1a Village Road, Oxton Village

Helen Jones opened Twenty Sixteen in October 2022 in Oxton Village, naming it after the year the family arrived on the Wirral. Within two years it had won the Good Food Award Blue Ribbon two consecutive times, the 2024/25 and 2025/26 awards, voted by customers rather than inspectors. The all-day menu runs from full breakfasts and eggs at nine through brunch and lunch until four, with homemade cakes and bakes from local bakers alongside a well-kept coffee counter. Everything is made fresh in house and the children's menu, activity packs and toy box make it genuinely family-friendly without the usual concessions in quality. Dog-friendly too, which says something about the neighbourhood.

Restaurant

Daise by Tony Parkin

Rose Mount, Oxton Village

Tony Parkin has earned Michelin stars at two separate restaurants - the Tudor Room at Great Fosters in Surrey and the House restaurant at Cliff House Hotel in Waterford - with a career that also includes Noma in Copenhagen, Restaurant Gordon Ramsay and Northcote. In February 2026 he opened Daise, his first solo restaurant, on Rose Mount in Oxton on the site of the legendary Fraiche. The room holds five tables and twelve diners; his wife Laura runs front of house. The tasting menu is shot through with well-judged Asian flavours - daikon, yuzu, kaffir lime alongside prime British produce - and the Michelin Guide added it to its Great Britain and Ireland selection in June 2026. Book well ahead.

Pub

Gallaghers Traditional Pub

20 Chester Street, Birkenhead town centre

The building at 20 Chester Street has been a pub since at least 1826, listed in Bagshaw's Directory of 1850 and trading under various names before Gallaghers rescued it from closure and reopened it as a free house in 2010. The interior is decorated with military memorabilia and a collection of Mersey shipping images - appropriate for a pub a short walk from the Woodside Ferry Terminal - and a mural on the side wall commemorates the sinking of HMS Birkenhead and the Birkenhead Drill. The beer is the point: a changing line-up of cask ales, craft beers and premium lagers, kept well and priced fairly. Wirral CAMRA Pub of the Season Autumn 2023 and Pub of Excellence 2024. Live music every Sunday from 7pm.

Birkenhead's Food Scene

Birkenhead's food map splits cleanly between the town centre and Oxton Village, the Georgian conservation area set on the ridge a mile inland. The town centre clusters around Hamilton Square - one of England's finest Georgian squares, with more Grade I listed buildings in one place than anywhere outside Trafalgar Square - and the Woodside Ferry Terminal, where the cross-Mersey service has run since the 1100s. The pubs and casual spots here serve the ferry crowd and the market-town regulars.

Oxton Village is where Birkenhead's independent food scene has quietly concentrated. The conservation village, a cluster of Victorian villas and cottages around a central green, now holds an unusually strong collection of independents: the bar-kitchen at Oxton Bar and Kitchen, the twice-award-winning Twenty Sixteen cafe, and since February 2026 Daise by Tony Parkin - a Michelin-listed tasting room on the site of the long-celebrated Fraiche. For a town of 90,000, this is a remarkable concentration of quality.

Birkenhead has always eaten well by necessity: it fed the shipyard workers of Cammell Laird, the market traders who have gathered at Birkenhead Market since 1835, and the Mersey ferry passengers coming and going from Woodside. The local signature dish is scouse, the hearty lamb and potato stew that gave Merseysiders their nickname. That working-class directness sits comfortably alongside Oxton Village's growing reputation as the Wirral's most interesting postcode for food.

Behind the Pass: The Kitchens That Keep Birkenhead Fed

Behind every bar in this guide is a kitchen, and behind every kitchen an extraction system - canopy, baffle filters, plenum, ductwork and fan - drawing grease-laden air off the cookline and out through the roof. Birkenhead's pub-town heritage and its clutch of independent bar-kitchens run hot stoves through long service windows, loading canopies with the kind of grease-laden vapour that a Wirral peninsula sea breeze alone will not clear.

Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the "responsible person" for a premises must assess and manage fire risk, and a grease-laden duct is one of the most serious risks in any catering building. The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) reinforce the same duty of care, while an Environmental Health Officer (EHO) or a buildings insurer can ask to see documented proof the extraction system has been professionally cleaned.

What is TR19 Grease?

TR19 Grease is the UK industry standard for cleaning ductwork and extraction systems in commercial kitchens, published by the Building Engineering Services Association (BESA). It defines how grease deposits are measured, the maximum thickness allowed before cleaning is due, and the post-clean certification a kitchen should hold - the benchmark insurers, fire assessors and EHOs look for.

A great pub kitchen lives or dies on its food. The extraction system is what keeps it open, insured and safe behind the scenes.

Industry Insight

The Cleaning Frequency Most Operators Get Wrong

Here is something many venue owners do not realise: under TR19 Grease, cleaning frequency is set by how many hours the kitchen actually cooks, not by how the canopy looks. A heavy-use kitchen running 12 to 16 hours a day should be cleaned roughly every three months. Moderate use of 6 to 12 hours points to every six months, and light use of 2 to 6 hours to every twelve.

The catch is that baffle filters can look clean while the plenum, the horizontal duct runs and the fan housing carry a heavy grease load that throttles airflow and lowers extract efficiency. A fire in that concealed ductwork spreads fast. That is why a dated TR19 certificate, kept in the fire logbook, is the document an insurer expects to see - and its absence at the point of a claim is a common reason cover is refused.

Planning Your Visit

Oxton Village is the natural hub for a food day: Twenty Sixteen for breakfast or brunch, Oxton Bar and Kitchen for lunch or afternoon drinks, and Daise in the evening if you have booked ahead. The village is on the 41 and 471 bus routes from Birkenhead town centre, or a 15-minute walk up the ridge. Parking is easy off Village Road.

From Oxton, the town centre is the second leg: Hamilton Square and its Georgian terraces, Birkenhead Market, and Gallaghers on Chester Street for a cask ale before the ferry back across the Mersey. Merseyrail runs to Hamilton Square and Birkenhead Park stations, and the Woodside Ferry adds a proper Mersey crossing to finish the day.

  • Book Daise by Tony Parkin well ahead - only five tables and twelve covers
  • Arrive at Twenty Sixteen before 11am on weekends - it fills quickly
  • Ask Gallaghers which cask ales are on that week before ordering
  • Oxton Bar and Kitchen is dog-friendly on the terrace in fine weather
  • Use Merseyrail to Hamilton Square, then the 41 bus up to Oxton Village

What to Order and How to Do the Day

Order with intent. At Oxton Bar and Kitchen, whatever local cask ale is on the handpump alongside a plate of Menai mussels or the char-grilled steak. At Twenty Sixteen, the full breakfast or a brunch plate with a proper filter coffee. At Daise, surrender to the tasting menu - the kitchen changes it constantly with the micro seasons, so the dish that arrives is the dish to trust. At Gallaghers, a pint of cask ale in the main bar with the shipping photographs for company.

Frequently Asked Questions

Birkenhead Food and Drink FAQ

Where is the best place to eat in Birkenhead?

Oxton Village is the strongest postcode for independent food, with the Oxton Bar and Kitchen, Twenty Sixteen Coffee and Kitchen, and Daise by Tony Parkin all within a short walk of each other in the conservation village.

Does Birkenhead have a Michelin restaurant?

Yes. Daise by Tony Parkin on Rose Mount in Oxton was added to the Michelin Guide Great Britain and Ireland in June 2026. Chef Parkin has previously held stars at the Tudor Room in Surrey and at Cliff House Hotel in Waterford, Ireland.

What is the local dish of Birkenhead?

Scouse is the traditional local dish - a hearty lamb and potato stew that gave Merseysiders their nickname. It is still served at several traditional venues around the town, including at Woodside Ferry Village.

What is Oxton Village and why does it matter for food?

Oxton is a Victorian conservation village on the Wirral ridge about a mile from Birkenhead town centre. It has become the Wirral's most concentrated independent food quarter, with award-winning cafes, an independent bar-kitchen and a Michelin-listed restaurant all within easy walking distance of each other.

Which is the best pub in Birkenhead for real ale?

Gallaghers Traditional Pub on Chester Street is a multiple CAMRA award winner - Wirral CAMRA Pub of the Season Autumn 2023 and Pub of Excellence 2024. It is a free house with a changing line-up of cask ales, close to the Woodside Ferry Terminal.

Can you do a Birkenhead food day on public transport?

Easily. Merseyrail serves Hamilton Square and Birkenhead Park stations in the town centre, from where the 41 bus climbs to Oxton Village. The Woodside Ferry provides a cross-Mersey return to Liverpool if you want to extend the day.
