Where to Eat in Stockport

Quick Answer

For a fast answer: Kambuja at the Produce Hall for rare Cambodian cooking in a buzzing market hall, Yón Orangé on the Underbanks for speciality coffee and homemade matcha cakes, Cantaloupe for a Michelin Bib Gourmand daily menu in a tiled wine-bar setting, and the Arden Arms for cask Robinsons ales under a curved Victorian bar in a coaching inn that dates to 1815. Four moods, one revived Old Town.

Rather than list everywhere in Stockport, this guide names four places worth going out of your way for, one in each everyday category, and lets them point you towards the rest of the town.

The Four Picks

Takeaway/Casual

Kambuja

Market Place, Stockport Produce Hall (SK1 1EU)

Kambuja is the only Cambodian restaurant in Northern England. Chef-founder Y Sok opened the original in Marple in October 2015 and brought the concept into Stockport's regenerated Produce Hall, the covered food hall that reopened inside the historic market complex. Everything is made fresh to order: the crispy tamarind ginger duck, the fish amok - Cambodia's national dish of coconut curry with lime leaf, lemongrass and spinach - and a deep roster of vegan and vegetarian Southeast Asian dishes. The Produce Hall setting, with its cocktail bar, indie soundtrack and communal benches, makes it one of the most enjoyable casual lunches in greater Manchester. A rare find this far north.

Yon Orange

57 Great Underbank, Stockport Old Town (SK1 1NE)

Yon Orange sits at the heart of the revived Underbanks, the medieval street that curves beneath Stockport's famous viaduct and has been transformed since 2018 into the town's most characterful independent quarter. Opened in 2025 by Neeve and Kallum, it occupies a small Victorian shopfront dressed in vintage-inspired decor, lush greenery and soft lighting that makes it feel like someone's very good living room. The speciality coffee is taken seriously - rotating single origins, careful espresso work - and the matcha programme is equally considered. Homemade cakes and vegan-friendly bakes complete the picture. It has quickly become the cafe the Underbanks needed.

Known for

Cantaloupe

71 Great Underbank, Stockport Old Town (SK1 1PE)

Co-owned by chefs Joshua Reed-Cooper and Mike Thomas - whose combined CVs take in Where The Light Gets In, Mana, The French and Hawksmoor - Cantaloupe is a 30-cover wine-bar restaurant in a tiled, shopfront space on the Underbanks. The menu changes daily, guided by what small trusted suppliers have in, and leans towards the French auberge and Italian trattoria: expect a pasta dish, perhaps some whole fish, bright Mediterranean flavours and a kitchen that knows when to step back and let ingredients speak. In February 2026 it was awarded a Michelin Bib Gourmand, the guide's mark for excellent cooking at honest prices, one of 37 new awards in the 2026 UK guide. Book ahead.

The Arden Arms

23 Millgate, Stockport Market Place (SK1 2LX)

The Arden Arms was built around 1815 on a site where an inn called Ye Blew Stoops had stood since at least 1709, making it one of the oldest continuously licensed houses in Stockport. Acquired by Robinsons Brewery in 1889, very little has changed inside since the early 1900s: CAMRA rates its interior as of outstanding national historic importance - three stars on the National Inventory of Historic Pub Interiors - for its curved glazed bar, hidden snug, chandeliers and long-case clock. The Grade II-listed building sits on the edge of the historic Market Place, and the pub has won both regional and national CAMRA Pub of the Year awards. Four regular Robinsons ales plus a changing guest; food served in the courtyard in season.

Stockport's Food Scene

Stockport's food story is inseparable from its architectural one. The town's historic core - the Underbanks, the Produce Hall inside the Victorian market, the viaduct arches and the warren of medieval streets around the Market Place - spent decades in decline before a sustained regeneration effort from around 2018 turned them into the most concentrated stretch of independent food and drink in Greater Manchester outside the city centre.

The Underbanks is the spine of the revival. The narrow, sloping street beneath the viaduct now holds a run of small independents including Cantaloupe, Yon Orange, the craft-beer bar the Spinn Off, and Where The Light Gets In - chef Sam Buckley's Michelin Green Star tasting-menu restaurant in a Victorian coffee warehouse at 7 Rostron Brow, opened in 2016, which won Restaurant of the Year at the Manchester Food and Drink Awards 2025. The Produce Hall adds a food-hall dimension above the market, hosting a roster of independent traders.

Stockport does not have a single signature dish the way Birmingham has the balti, but it has a strong civic identity around its hat-making heritage and viaduct skyline, and its food scene reflects a town that has chosen quality and independence over volume. The result is a place where a Michelin Bib Gourmand and a CAMRA National Inventory pub sit within five minutes' walk of each other.

Behind the Pass: The Kitchens That Keep Stockport Fed

Every kitchen in this guide, however small, runs an extraction system - canopy, baffle filters, plenum, ductwork and fan - that pulls heat, smoke and grease-laden air off the cookline. Stockport's Produce Hall and Underbanks kitchens run back-to-back services through a long weekend, from lunchtime street food to late-night small plates, and every busy session pushes grease-laden vapour into canopies above woks, plancha grills and fryers running at full stretch.

Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the "responsible person" for a premises must assess and manage fire risk, and a grease-laden duct is one of the most serious risks in any catering building. The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) reinforce the same duty of care, while an Environmental Health Officer (EHO) or a buildings insurer can ask to see documented proof the extraction system has been professionally cleaned.

What is TR19 Grease?

TR19 Grease is the UK industry standard for cleaning ductwork and extraction systems in commercial kitchens, published by the Building Engineering Services Association (BESA). It defines how grease deposits are measured, the maximum thickness allowed before cleaning is due, and the post-clean certification a kitchen should hold - the benchmark insurers, fire assessors and EHOs look for.

A small kitchen is no less of a fire risk than a big one. A clean, certified extraction system is what keeps it safe and insured.

Industry Insight

The Cleaning Frequency Most Operators Get Wrong

Here is something many venue owners do not realise: under TR19 Grease, cleaning frequency is set by how many hours the kitchen actually cooks, not by how the canopy looks. A heavy-use kitchen running 12 to 16 hours a day should be cleaned roughly every three months. Moderate use of 6 to 12 hours points to every six months, and light use of 2 to 6 hours to every twelve.

The catch is that baffle filters can look clean while the plenum, the horizontal duct runs and the fan housing carry a heavy grease load that throttles airflow and lowers extract efficiency. A fire in that concealed ductwork spreads fast. That is why a dated TR19 certificate, kept in the fire logbook, is the document an insurer expects to see - and its absence at the point of a claim is a common reason cover is refused.

Planning Your Visit

Almost everything in this guide sits within ten minutes on foot. The Underbanks, Cantaloupe, Yon Orange and Where The Light Gets In are clustered in Stockport Old Town; the Produce Hall is inside the Market Place complex a short walk uphill; and the Arden Arms is at the Market Place edge on Millgate. Stockport station is a 12-minute train ride from Manchester Piccadilly, making this a realistic half-day trip from the city.

The Underbanks slope means the street feels different depending on which end you enter from. Start from the Market Place end at the Arden Arms, work down through Yon Orange and Cantaloupe, and finish with a booking at Where The Light Gets In for the evening. The Produce Hall is best for a midday session - Kambuja serves from 11am and the bar opens on Fridays and Saturdays.

  • Start at the Arden Arms - arrive early and take time with the Victorian interior
  • Walk the Underbanks from the Market Place end; peek into every shopfront
  • Book Cantaloupe ahead - 30 covers fill quickly on weekends
  • Try the fish amok at Kambuja in the Produce Hall at lunchtime
  • Book Where The Light Gets In weeks ahead for a special evening

What to Order and How to Do the Day

Order with intent. At Kambuja, the fish amok - Cambodia's national dish, coconut curry with lime leaf and lemongrass - and the crispy tamarind ginger duck. At Yon Orange, ask what single-origin espresso is on, and pair it with whatever homemade cake came out of the kitchen that morning. At Cantaloupe, trust the daily menu and the wine list - the kitchen is at its best when left to lead. At the Arden Arms, a pint of Robinsons Best Bitter or Trooper taken slowly at the curved bar.

Frequently Asked Questions

Stockport Food and Drink FAQ

What is the best restaurant in Stockport?

Where The Light Gets In at 7 Rostron Brow, a Michelin Green Star tasting-menu restaurant in a Victorian coffee warehouse, is widely regarded as Stockport's flagship table. Chef Sam Buckley opened it in 2016 and it won Restaurant of the Year at the Manchester Food and Drink Awards 2025. Book well ahead - it is open Wednesday to Saturday evenings and Saturday lunch only.

Where is the Underbanks in Stockport?

The Underbanks is the historic sloping street in Stockport Old Town, running beneath the town's famous Victorian railway viaduct from the Market Place down to the river. It holds a cluster of independent cafes, restaurants and bars including Yon Orange, Cantaloupe and the Spinn Off, and has been regenerated since around 2018 into the town's most characterful food quarter.

Does Stockport have a Michelin restaurant?

Yes - two notable ones. Where The Light Gets In holds a Michelin Green Star for sustainable gastronomy and features in the Michelin Guide. Cantaloupe on the Underbanks was awarded a Michelin Bib Gourmand in February 2026 for excellent quality at honest prices.

What is the best pub in Stockport for real ale?

The Arden Arms on Millgate, a Grade II-listed coaching inn built around 1815, is Stockport's most celebrated ale pub. CAMRA rates its interior as of outstanding national historic importance and it has won regional and national CAMRA Pub of the Year awards. It serves four regular Robinsons ales and a changing guest.

Is Stockport worth visiting for food?

Yes. The Underbanks and Produce Hall have made Stockport one of the most concentrated independent food destinations in Greater Manchester. A short 12-minute train from Manchester Piccadilly gives you a Michelin-recognised restaurant, a Bib Gourmand wine bar, a Cambodian street-food stall and a Victorian pub of national historic importance, all within ten minutes on foot.

How do I get to Stockport town centre from Manchester?

Stockport station is 12 minutes by train from Manchester Piccadilly and is served by Northern and Avanti West Coast services. The station exit brings you into the town centre a short walk from the Market Place, the Underbanks and the Produce Hall.
