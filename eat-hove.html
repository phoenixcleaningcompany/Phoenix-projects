Where to Eat in Hove

Quick Answer

For a fast answer: Bayon Bakery on Hove Park Villas for an artisan pastry and queue-worthy weekend croissant, Wolfox Lab on Western Road for the south coast's only organic-certified roastery flat white, Fourth and Church on Church Road for Sam Pryor's BRAVO-winning small plates and wine, and The Urchin on Belfast Street for fresh shellfish and a pint of their own Larrikin brew. Four moods, one compact seaside town.

Rather than list everywhere in Hove, this guide names four places worth going out of your way for, one in each everyday category, and lets them point you towards the rest of the town.

The Four Picks

Bakery

Bayon Bakery

11 Hove Park Villas, Hove Park

Amber Sterck opened Bayon Bakery at Hove Park Villas in September 2024, and within months the neighbourhood bakehouse had acquired a reputation well beyond its postcode. The draw is a daily run of hand-shaped sourdough loaves, classic baguettes, almond croissants and pain au chocolat - all baked in-house from scratch each morning alongside a short menu of sandwiches and soup. On Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays Bayon releases a filled weekend croissant in a rotating flavour, and by mid-morning the queue has wound around the block. The ethos, captured in the slogan 'simple pleasures, done well', is resolutely local: good coffee, warm staff and a small interior where regulars linger over a flat white and a pastry. No franchise, no formula - just serious baking in a residential Hove backstreet.

Known for
Weekend filled croissants and daily sourdough baked from scratch
Good for

Cafe

Wolfox Lab

109 Western Road, Brunswick Town

Fabio Lauro founded Wolfox in Hove in 2017 as an organic-first speciality roaster, and the Lab on Western Road is where the philosophy is most visible. Wolfox holds Soil Association certification as the UK's only fully organic-certified coffee roastery - every bean sourced, roasted and served to organic standards - a claim no other British roaster can match. The cafe is a calm, design-led room in the heart of Brunswick Town, serving espresso, filter and matcha alongside pastries, with flat whites made to the same standard the wholesale operation holds itself to. Head of coffee Anderson Locatelli has shaped the palate since 2018, and Wolfox's beans now supply cafes in London, Leeds and Madrid.

Known for
The UK's only Soil Association-certified organic coffee roastery, open in Hove since 2017
Good for
A considered flat white or filter in a neighbourhood roastery-cafe with genuine credentials
Restaurant

Fourth and Church

84 Church Road, Hove

Sam Pryor trained at Heston Blumenthal's Fat Duck before moving into consultancy; Paul Morgan ran the kitchen at terre a terre before managing Lewes's Pelham House Hotel. Together they opened Fourth and Church on Church Road in October 2015 as a neighbourhood restaurant, wine bar and independent wine shop. The menu is seasonal and ingredient-led - small sharing plates with meats and vegetables often finished over charcoal in the evenings - alongside snacks, charcuterie and British artisan cheese. The wine list earned BRAVO Best Wine List in both 2023 and 2024; the BRAVO 2025 judges voted it Best Lunch in Brighton and Hove. Grace Dent called it a small, independent labour of love in The Guardian. Book ahead for evenings.

Known for
BRAVO Best Wine List 2023 and 2024, Best Lunch 2025 - charcoal-finished small plates on Church Road
Good for
A long lunch or evening of small plates and excellent wine in Hove's best restaurant street
Pub

The Urchin

15-17 Belfast Street, Hove

The Urchin took over a Belfast Street pub and became a freehouse in 2016, building a reputation for the pairing that defines it: simple, fresh shellfish and well-kept craft beer. In August 2018 co-owner Nick installed a microbrewery in the basement, and the house beer Larrikin - named for the Australian slang for a loveable rogue - has been on the pumps ever since alongside rotating Sussex guest ales. The kitchen is refreshingly focused: oysters, mussels, king prawns, crab cakes, moules mariniere, scallops and lobster, sourced for freshness and served without fuss. The combination of house-brewed beer and serious shellfish has made it a consistent fixture in the Top 50 Gastropubs listing.

Hove's Food Scene

Hove's food geography runs along three roughly parallel axes. Church Road is the main event: a mile-long independent restaurant strip stretching from the town hall to the Seven Dials junction, where within a twenty-minute walk you pass a Michelin-starred tasting menu, a Bib Gourmand Italian, a Persian charcoal grill and Fourth and Church's wine-forward small plates. It is, by any measure, one of the strongest independent restaurant streets in the south of England.

Parallel to it runs Western Road, where the shops and cafes shade into the handsome Regency squares of Brunswick Town - Brunswick Square and Adelaide Crescent - a neighbourhood whose wide pavements and terrace-fronted cafes give Hove its particular unhurried character. Wolfox Lab sits here, and the George Street pedestrian lane, a short block off Western Road, holds a dense run of bistros, wine bars and boutiques. Back from the seafront, Belfast Street and the streets running down to Hove Lawns are home to the more neighbourhood-facing venues, The Urchin among them.

Hove has no single signature dish - its identity is more coastal-eclectic - but Sussex produces inform the better kitchens across the strip: Brighton and Hove fishmongers, local dairy from the South Downs, seasonal vegetables from the weald, and the shellfish that the Channel still delivers in quantity. The town's food scene has grown fastest in the 2020s, with Church Road now holding three tasting-menu restaurants on one short stretch, a density that would be notable anywhere.

Behind the Pass: The Kitchens That Keep Hove Fed

Fryers, grills and pass after pass through a busy season put a commercial kitchen's extraction system - canopy, baffle filters, plenum, ductwork and fan - under real strain, drawing grease-laden vapour out of the building. Hove's kitchens run through a long summer season, from the seafront fryers along Hove Lawns to the back-to-back services along Church Road, and the grease load accumulating in their canopies through those months is as heavy as anywhere on the south coast.

Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the "responsible person" for a premises must assess and manage fire risk, and a grease-laden duct is one of the most serious risks in any catering building. The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) reinforce the same duty of care, while an Environmental Health Officer (EHO) or a buildings insurer can ask to see documented proof the extraction system has been professionally cleaned.

What is TR19 Grease?

TR19 Grease is the UK industry standard for cleaning ductwork and extraction systems in commercial kitchens, published by the Building Engineering Services Association (BESA). It defines how grease deposits are measured, the maximum thickness allowed before cleaning is due, and the post-clean certification a kitchen should hold - the benchmark insurers, fire assessors and EHOs look for.

A seaside kitchen earns its year in a few hot months. A clean extraction system is what keeps it trading through every one of them.

Industry Insight

The Cleaning Frequency Most Operators Get Wrong

Here is something many venue owners do not realise: under TR19 Grease, cleaning frequency is set by how many hours the kitchen actually cooks, not by how the canopy looks. A heavy-use kitchen running 12 to 16 hours a day should be cleaned roughly every three months. Moderate use of 6 to 12 hours points to every six months, and light use of 2 to 6 hours to every twelve.

The catch is that baffle filters can look clean while the plenum, the horizontal duct runs and the fan housing carry a heavy grease load that throttles airflow and lowers extract efficiency. A fire in that concealed ductwork spreads fast. That is why a dated TR19 certificate, kept in the fire logbook, is the document an insurer expects to see - and its absence at the point of a claim is a common reason cover is refused.

Planning Your Visit

The four venues spread across Hove but none is far from any other. Wolfox Lab on Western Road and Fourth and Church on Church Road are a ten-minute walk apart, with the George Street pedestrian lane running between them. The Urchin on Belfast Street is a further ten minutes south toward the seafront. Bayon Bakery on Hove Park Villas is the outlier, a short bus ride or twenty-minute walk north into the residential streets above New Church Road.

Done as a day: a morning at Bayon for a pastry and coffee, a flat white at Wolfox on Western Road while browsing the Brunswick antique shops, a long lunch at Fourth and Church, then an evening at The Urchin - a pint of Larrikin with a plateau of shellfish before the walk back along the seafront. Hove Station is a five-minute walk from Church Road, and the seafront is ten minutes on foot from there.

  • Visit Bayon Bakery early on a weekend - the filled croissants sell out fast
  • Order a filter at Wolfox Lab and ask which single origin is on that week
  • Book Fourth and Church ahead for evenings - the wine list rewards asking for recommendations
  • Arrive at The Urchin hungry: order the oysters, mussels and a pint of Larrikin
  • Walk Church Road end to end to take in the full range of Hove independents

What to Order and How to Do the Day

Order with intent. At Bayon, whatever filled croissant is on that weekend and a flat white to go with it. At Wolfox, the single-origin filter or a house flat white made with their own organic roast. At Fourth and Church, lean into the sharing plates and let the team guide the wine - the by-the-glass list is a highlight. At The Urchin, order the oysters to start, a bowl of moules mariniere as a main, and a pint of Larrikin beside both.

Frequently Asked Questions

Hove Food and Drink FAQ

Where is the best independent cafe in Hove?

Wolfox Lab at 109 Western Road in Brunswick Town is the standout, operating as the UK's only Soil Association-certified organic coffee roastery since 2017. Founded by Fabio Lauro, it roasts to organic standards and serves espresso, filter and matcha in a calm, design-led neighbourhood cafe.

What is the best restaurant on Church Road Hove?

Fourth and Church at number 84 is among the most celebrated, having won BRAVO Best Wine List in Brighton and Hove in both 2023 and 2024, and Best Lunch in 2025. Sam Pryor and Paul Morgan have run the neighbourhood restaurant and wine bar since October 2015.

Does Hove have a pub with its own brewery?

Yes. The Urchin on Belfast Street installed a microbrewery in its basement in August 2018 and brews its own Larrikin beer on site, available on tap in the bar alongside guest Sussex ales. It is a freehouse specialising in fresh shellfish.

Where can I get the best pastries in Hove?

Bayon Bakery at 11 Hove Park Villas, opened in September 2024 by Amber Sterck, bakes sourdough, croissants and pain au chocolat from scratch each morning. The weekend filled croissant in rotating flavours draws queues around the block.

What are Hove's best food streets?

Church Road is the main restaurant strip, running from the town hall to Seven Dials with independent venues the entire length. George Street, a pedestrianised lane off Western Road, holds a dense run of bistros and wine bars. Western Road through Brunswick Town has the neighbourhood cafes.

Can you do a Hove food day on foot?

Easily. Wolfox Lab and Fourth and Church are a ten-minute walk apart via Western Road and Church Road. The Urchin on Belfast Street is ten minutes south toward the seafront. Bayon Bakery on Hove Park Villas is best as a morning start before heading down to Western Road, or reached by bus from the centre.
