# Pub Cold Outreach — 3-Email Sequence

Same format/reasoning as the Wales/England care home sequence: plain text, no images or attachments, one topic per email, soft CTA. Source list: manually gathered emails from CAMRA/WhatPub listings.

`[Pub Name]` is a manual placeholder in the subject line only — fill in if you're tracking pub names against emails, or drop it if not; the body doesn't need it either way.

Cadence: Email 1 → wait 10-14 days, no reply → Email 2 → wait 10-14 days, no reply → Email 3. Stop the moment someone replies or asks to be left alone.

## Email 1 — "What your insurer checks after a kitchen fire claim"

Subject: What insurers check after a kitchen fire claim — [Pub Name]

Hi there,

I'm Damien, I run Phoenix Duct Clean — TR19 kitchen extraction cleaning for pubs and commercial kitchens across the UK, 15+ years doing it. Thought this might be worth knowing regardless of whether we ever work together.

Ten things that commonly get checked after a commercial kitchen fire claim:

1. A valid, in-date TR19 grease extraction certificate
2. A fire risk assessment that's actually been reviewed recently, not just filed away
3. Records showing when the extraction system was last properly cleaned, and by whom
4. Fire doors in working order, not wedged open
5. Fire extinguishers and fire blankets present, in date, and positioned correctly near cooking equipment
6. Staff fire safety training records
7. Any fitted fire suppression system serviced within its required interval
8. Portable appliance (PAT) testing up to date
9. No unrecorded alterations to kitchen equipment or ventilation since the last inspection
10. Evidence that previous fire risk assessment recommendations were actually acted on

Number 1 and 3 are where I come in — a lot of pubs have someone wipe the canopy down periodically, which isn't the same thing as a certified TR19 clean, and it's the certificate insurers specifically look for.

Following up from my last note — a different angle this time.

Worth two minutes checking your lease if you're not certain. If it turns out it's on you, or if it's just easier for you to sort directly rather than chase a landlord, I'm happy to quote either way. Last email from me on this — after this I'll leave it, no more chasing.

A lot of pubs are older buildings, sometimes listed, often with timber structure close to where the kitchen extraction runs. That's not a reason to worry, but it is a reason the extraction system matters more than it would in a modern flat-roofed unit — grease-laden ductwork is already a well-documented way a small kitchen fire spreads fast, and it spreads faster still through older, tighter, timber-framed spaces.
